## The question

- **Cap.** Josh's source files keep at most 1,000 cells per cell type and species (the "1000-cap"; `josh@main:tabula_make_samples.ipynb#c3`, `#c10` [C]; described in nb19#c2 [D]; [F08](F08-hvg-flavor-matrix.md)). Does training on the uncapped atlas change the CD8 holdout result? The stated aim was the "causal effect of upstream data-set size", with everything else "byte-identical" (nb19#c0) [D]. The 2026-05-26 mentor-meeting briefing ranked "improving size and balancedness" as the first thing to try (MB §2) [D].
- **M1 vs M2.** A holdout is a cell type removed from training; the model must predict it. M2 holds out non-classical monocytes (CL:0000875) plus the generic "monocyte" label (CL:0000576); M1 holds out only CL:0000875. Both names come from April ([F07](F07-toggle-iid-ood-and-renorm.md)). The 248 real human M2 test cells were two populations: 203 lung non-classical monocytes and 45 generic monocytes (43 bone marrow, 2 heart) (nbU#c7) [N]. M1 removes the second group. Switching "raised R² but did *not* tighten MMD" (nb21#c0) [D]. Why?
- **Reading MMD.** A raw MMD value had no reference point. What is the best possible value, what does "doing nothing" score, and which single number should rank models (CF:515-517, 811-847) [D]?

**Models.** Every run uses the same settings. The baseline called "scGen" is CellOT's plain autoencoder (AE: a network that compresses each cell to 50 numbers, "encode", and rebuilds it, "decode"), 1000→256→256→50, dropout 0.1, no KL term, plus a mean shift in the 50-d code. IMPACT_CellOT trains CellOT's input-convex network (4 layers of 64) to move mouse codes to human codes in that AE's space and decodes the result. Both run 50,000 iterations; IMPACT uses batch 128 (RES/hvg_pearson_residuals_m1_v08_ood/{scgen,impact_cellot}/config.yaml; the CD8 v08 config differs only in holdout and file paths) [C]. The CellOT paper trained for 250k iterations with batch 256 [P] (cellot.txt, Methods, "Hyperparameters" and the paragraph before "CellOT network architecture"). See `conny:docs/archaeology/models.md` for how this differs from the scGen paper. The CellOT code sets no training seed (no `manual_seed` or `np.random.seed` anywhere under `conny:cellot/cellot_gpu`) [C]. One training run exists per model and cut (RES folder listing) [R].

**Evaluation.** All numbers below are meant to be in gene space ("data space"), on the scored held-out cells; §1 shows one that is not. `evals.csv` draws n = 30, 50 and 80 cells from each cloud (set by `--n_cells 30,50,80` in the job scripts; the default is 100–1,500), 10 times, with pandas `sample()` and no seed (`conny:cellot/cellot_gpu/scripts/evaluate.py:196-204`; SB/eval_dataspace/*.sbatch) [C]. nb20 and nb22 report R² as the mean of the 10 squared r values; nb19 squares the mean r. Here the two differ by at most 0.0003 [C][R] (nb19#c24, nb20#c15, nb22#c1; recomputed from the `evals.csv` files). The metric sidecars written from 2026-06-02 use NumPy's generator with seed 0 (`conny:cellot/cellot_gpu/cellot/losses/mmd.py:13-91`) [C]. MMD uses all 1,000 genes (`conny:cellot/cellot_gpu/scripts/evaluate.py:78-80`) [C]; the CellOT paper used the top-50 marker genes [P] (cellot.txt, Methods, "Feature selection"). Notation: T = real human held-out cells; C = mouse held-out cells (the model's input); P = predicted cells; E/D = encode/decode.

| metric (file) | formula | one-line intuition |
|---|---|---|
| R² (`r2-means` in `evals.csv`, squared in notebooks) | mean over 10 draws of corr(μ_T, μ_P)², μ = per-gene mean of the n cells | does the average expression profile match? |
| MMD (`mmd`) | mean over 50 bandwidths γ ∈ logspace(1, −3, 50) of the biased MMD² below | how different are the two clouds as whole distributions? |
| `mmd_floor` | MMD between two disjoint random n-cell subsets of T | the score of a perfect prediction at this n |
| `mmd_ceiling` | MMD(C, T) | the score of doing nothing |
| `gap_above_floor` | MMD(P, T) − floor | error left after removing the sample-size offset |
| `frac_gap_closed` ("fgc_raw") | (ceiling − MMD(P, T)) / (ceiling − floor) | share of the do-nothing → perfect distance covered; < 0 means worse than doing nothing |
| `mmd_ae_recon_floor` ("AE floor") | MMD(D(E(T)), T) | what a perfect transport could score after decoding |
| `mmd_decoded_ceiling` | MMD(D(E(C)), T) | doing nothing in the code space, then decoding |
| `frac_gap_closed_decoded` ("fgc_dec") | (dec. ceiling − MMD(P, T)) / (dec. ceiling − AE floor) | the same share, with both references passed through the AE |
| `frac_r2_closed` | (r2_model − r2_identity) / (r2_self − r2_identity); r2_self = corr² of means of two random halves of T (10 splits), r2_identity = corr²(μ_C, μ_T), all on full pools | share of the mean-expression gap closed |
| `frac_r2_closed_decoded` | the same with T, C replaced by D(E(T)), D(E(C)) | — |
| `model_over_floor` (added 2026-07-30) | MMD(P, T) / AE floor | how many times worse than a perfect decoded prediction |
| `mean_js` | mean over genes of the Jensen–Shannon divergence (a symmetric, bounded form of KL divergence) between 50-bin histograms of T and P | do single-gene distributions match? 0 = same, ln 2 = disjoint |

Sources: `conny:cellot/cellot_gpu/scripts/extended_metrics.py:136-189`, `conny:cellot/cellot_gpu/scripts/decoded_frame_metrics.py:124-157`, `conny:cellot/cellot_gpu/cellot/losses/divergence.py:29-80`, nb22#c1 (the R² recipe), `conny:scripts/eval_external_target.py:22` [C]. In the MMD part of the decoded frame only the references are decoded and T stays raw. In the R² part T is decoded too: `r2_model_dec` compares the prediction's gene means with those of D(E(T)), not of the real cells (`conny:cellot/cellot_gpu/scripts/decoded_frame_metrics.py:129-138`) [C].

**The MMD estimator is biased.** With k(a, b) = exp(−γ‖a − b‖²), the code computes MMD²_b = (1/n²) Σᵢ Σⱼ k(xᵢ, xⱼ) + (1/m²) Σᵢ Σⱼ k(yᵢ, yⱼ) − (2/nm) Σᵢ Σⱼ k(xᵢ, yⱼ) on full kernel matrices (`conny:cellot/cellot_gpu/cellot/losses/mmd.py:5-10`) [C]. The first two sums include each cell compared with itself, where k = 1. For two samples of the same distribution the expected value is (1/n)(1 − E k(x, x′)) + (1/m)(1 − E k(y, y′)): positive, and shrinking like 1/n [I]. When γ is large, two different cells look unrelated (k ≈ 0) and the value tends to 2/n. Measured on M2 v07: floor 0.0637, 0.0381, 0.0240 at n = 30, 50, 80, against 2/n = 0.0667, 0.0400, 0.0250 (nb20#c10) [N]. The positive floor comes from the self-comparison terms. The off-diagonal terms subtract (1/n + 1/m)·E k, which is why the measured floor sits at about 96% of 2/n; that implies an average k between two different cells of about 0.04–0.05 over the 50 bandwidths [I]. The CellOT paper says it reports "an unbiased estimate" [P] (cellot.txt, Methods, "Evaluation metrics"). The signal sits at middle bandwidths: on M2 v07 the per-γ excess over the floor peaks at γ ≈ 0.0054 (IMPACT) and 0.0066 (baseline) (nb20#c3) [N]; at large γ every comparison tends to 2/n (nb20#c9) [D].

### 3. Smart-seq2 and the assay filter (2026-06-05)

- "Scattered" cells were defined as the 15% farthest from their species' centre in PCA space: 64 scattered, 362 "blob" (nb21#c7) [N]. Scattered-vs-blob marker genes had adjusted p-values of about 1 (the lowest of the 24 shown is 0.085) (nb21#c9) [N].

One detached 32-cell Leiden cluster is 100% Smart-seq2 (nb21#c12) [N]. The "donor effect" in §4 of the notebook was this platform effect (nb21#c13) [D]. The docs' "~77%" (nb21#c13; CF:865) matches the output [N].

### 4. The AE round-trip and the decoded frame (2026-06-05 → 06-09)

The repo's term `AE round-trip tax` means: P has passed through decode, T has not, and decoding alone moves cells away from real cells (CF:698-745) [D]. Measured for M1 v08 at n = 80: MMD(D(E(T)), T) = 0.0805, against a raw floor of 0.0236 (RES/hvg_pearson_residuals_m1_v08_ood/impact_cellot/evals_ood_data_space/decoded_frame_metrics.csv and extended_metrics.csv) [R]. `decoded_frame_metrics.py` (added 2026-06-09 13:22 in `conny@7c178c5`) [G] scores both references through the AE. ### 7. Do the metrics behave sensibly?

- **Sample-size offset: handled.** Subtracting the floor makes the error flat across n (M2 v07 IMPACT gap 0.081, 0.084, 0.082 at n = 30, 50, 80; nb20#c10) [N]. Raw MMD values are only comparable at equal n.
- **Raw mouse cells score almost as well as IMPACT on the decoded scale.** With the raw mouse cells passed in as the "prediction", M1 v08 scores fgc_dec 0.874 and `model_over_floor` 1.35, against 0.897 and 1.29 for IMPACT [R] (EXT/_selftest_m1_v08_nulltransport, _selftest_m1_v08_impact). The decoded mouse cells score 0.000 (by construction) and 3.80 (= decoded ceiling / AE floor) [R]. The difference between IMPACT and raw mouse, 0.005 in MMD, is close to the re-draw noise below (up to 0.0033) [R][I]. The committed script warns when a prediction has many exact zeros (`conny:scripts/eval_external_target.py:145-163`, already in `conny@4244985`) [C][G]. The saved raw-mouse test was written at 20:27 UTC, before the two other self-tests (20:28 and 20:32); its JSON has no zero-fraction fields and an empty `warnings` list, so it was probably made before the check existed [R][I]. With 84% zeros in the prediction against 76% in the target, the committed check would fire [R][I]. RB:622-625 says the script "correctly flags a raw-frame prediction" [D]; no saved run shows this.
- **fgc_dec can exceed 1.** The CD8 uncapped v07 baseline scores 1.093 at n = 30, because its MMD (0.1474) is below the AE floor (0.1524) (RES/hvg_pearson_residuals_a_ood_uncapped/scgen/evals_ood_data_space/decoded_frame_metrics.csv) [R]. The margin is small (0.005, at n = 30 only) [R]. A later run scores 1.032 (log0730:333; [F16](F16-deployment-models-and-mixhvg.md)) [D]. The "floor" is not a lower bound: a prediction can land closer to the real cells than the AE's own reconstruction does [I].
- **Small denominators.** Decoded ceiling − floor at n = 80: 0.186 and 0.179 (M1, M2 v07), 0.225 (M1, M2 v08), 0.146 (CD8 v08), 0.064 (CD8 v07) (decoded sidecars) [R]; 0.020 on an LPS cut (`fasrc:BK/scgen-cellot-ablation/results/transport_calibration/lps_transport.csv`, row `raw_prediction`) [R], 0.022 in CF:634 [D]. Two independent draws of the same predictions differ by up to 0.0033 MMD at n = 80 (`evals.csv` vs sidecar for the 11 runs that have both; largest for the CD8 v07 baseline, 0.1163 vs 0.1196; median 0.0009) [R]. That moves the fraction by about ±0.015 on the v08 M cuts, ±0.05 on CD8 v07 and ±0.17 on LPS [I]. The same model's fgc_dec also moves with n: 0.956, 0.900, 0.935 (CD8 v08 IMPACT) and 1.093, 0.992, 0.911 (CD8 v07 baseline) [R].
- **Pure noise scored +0.52.** RB:484-501 reports that synthetic random counts gave fgc_dec +0.52 for one model and −0.23 to +0.21 for three others, with `model_over_floor` 4.3–9.3 [D] (added in `conny@f5a98d5`) [G]. No result file was found (EXT holds only the three M1 self-tests) [R], and the target used is not recorded. The fraction measures improvement over the decoded input, so garbage moved halfway toward the target scores 0.5 [I].
- **mean_js mostly measures decoding.** M1 v08: IMPACT 0.449, AE floor 0.418, decoded mouse 0.460, raw mouse 0.090 (EXT/_selftest_m1_v08_*/external_target_metrics.csv) [R]. AGENTS.md lists it as a `guardrail` (a secondary check read next to fgc_dec) (`conny:AGENTS.md:35-37`) [D], but it hardly separates models; RB:564-567 says this later [D].
- **Cross-cut ranking.** Each cut and model set has its own AE, so its own ruler (CF:640-642) [D]. The CD8 v07 baseline (0.911) outranks every v08 IMPACT run except CD8 (0.935) on the scorecard's sort key [R][C].

## Verdict

- **Solid.** The MMD numbers in this repo are biased V-statistics that carry a sample-size offset of about 2/n (0.064 at n = 30, 0.024 at n = 80); the floor framework measures and removes it correctly [N][C]. Within one cut, IMPACT has lower MMD than the baseline in all eight gene-space pairs on this page (capped CD8, M1 v07, M2 v07 OOD and IID, the three v08 cuts and the CD8 IID twin), and the baseline is not better on fgc_dec anywhere [R]. Decoding accounts for a large part of IMPACT's distance from the real cells: about 71% (M1), 46% (M2) and 92% (CD8) of its excess over the raw floor on v08 [I], and the AE keeps only 5–22% of per-gene spread on held-out cells [R]. These are single runs, but the IMPACT–baseline MMD gaps (0.02–0.07) are 6–21 times the largest re-draw difference (0.0033) [R][I]; training-seed noise is unknown.
- **Not shown.** That removing the cap helps IMPACT (the comparison mixes spaces). That the assay filter improved the models (everything changed at once, including half of the genes, and raw MMD got worse in 3 of 4 pairs). Why M1 v07 was worse than M2 v07 (both frames agree it was, but the two cuts have different target cells; no cause was isolated). That Smart-seq2 cells were what kept M1's MMD high (never re-scored without them). Whether any result survives a second training seed.
- **The headline metric.** fgc_dec answers "how much better than decoded mouse cells is the output, judged against real human cells?" It does not answer "is the output close to real human cells?". On M1 v08 the untransported raw mouse cells are as close (MMD 0.109 vs 0.104) and score 0.874 on it [R]. Calling the raw fraction "dishonest" (nb22#c9) [D] goes too far: it is the right number for the second question [I]. IMPACT clearly improves mean expression (R² 0.92 vs 0.55 for raw mouse; EXT JSON `r2_all_genes`) [R], not the whole distribution.

## Problems in how it was done

- **Wrong space in the cap comparison.** The uncapped IMPACT score is a latent-space score (§1) [C][R]. nb19, the v08 scorecard and the v07→v08 deltas (CD8 IMPACT R² +0.073, MMD +0.090; nb22#c5) [N] all treat it as gene space.
- **Many changes at once.** v08 changes cells, test split, genes and the AE together [C]. For M1, only 524 of 1,000 genes and 99 of 183 scored human cells carry over from v07 (§5) [R]. No cut changes only one thing: there is no capped CD8 cut with the filter, and no cut with only the filter or only the stratified split (RES listing) [R]. So no effect can be attributed to the assay filter.
- **More data at the same compute.** Capped and uncapped models both trained for 50,000 steps [C] (HO:75 [D]), so the uncapped run traded passes per cell for more cells [I]. Removing the cap is confounded with this, and with a new test set (§1).
- **Cross-cut comparisons.** "M1 raised R² but not MMD" compares MMD across two cuts with different target cells (207 lung cells vs 248 cells including 45 from bone marrow and heart) [N], different genes (about 93% shared, nbU#c22 [D]) and different AEs (the `ae_emb` paths in the configs) [C]. Raw MMD has no common reference across cuts; equal floors (0.024) rule out only the sample-size part [N][I].
- **One run, no seeds.** No training seed; `evals.csv` draws unseeded [C]. Every comparison is between single runs [R]. CF:507-513 says so for M2 [D].
- **Small, overlapping test draws.** In the capped cuts, n = 80 cells are drawn from pools of 93–261 cells, so the 10 draws share many cells and their spread understates uncertainty [I].
- **Mixed draws in one table.** The scorecard puts unseeded `evals.csv` R² and MMD next to fractions computed from seeded sidecar draws (nb22#c1, #c3) [C]. The two MMD estimates differ by up to 0.0033 (§7) [R], so a row's MMD and fgc_raw do not match exactly.
- **Analyses on the wrong cells.** nb21.1, nb21.2 and the outlier script analyse a different half of the v08 holdout (92 of 183 cells shared) [R].
- **Unrun notebooks with written conclusions.** nb21.2 (error, unexecuted cells) and nb21 §7 (unexecuted) [N] state results anyway [D].
- **Docs disagree with results.** fgc_dec ≈ 0.91 vs 0.897; AE floor ≈ 0.083 vs 0.0805 [D][R]. The doc values already appear in the handoff dated 2026-06-05 (HO:109) [D], before the saved sidecar was written (2026-06-09) [R]; the AE-floor gap (0.0025) is within re-draw noise, so the docs look stale rather than based on a different method [I]. The docs' explanation of the raw-ceiling rise (Smart-seq2 removal) is stated as fact but untested (§5) [D][I]. `AE round-trip tax.md:38` writes the prediction as decode(encode(treated) + transport), which is wrong (it is decode(transport(encode(mouse)))) [D][C]. nb20#c14's "overshoot" reading was left in place [D]. nb19#c0 calls the pipeline "byte-identical" although its matcher is a re-implementation [D][C].
- **Single-draw baselines.** The "baseline to beat" (0.862 vs 0.869) is one draw and reverses the 10-draw order [R].
- **A ruler that rewards the input.** fgc_dec rates raw mouse cells at 0.874 [R]; the hub still ranks all runs across cuts and AEs on it [C], against CF:640-642 [D].
- **Paper protocol not followed.** MMD on 1,000 genes instead of the paper's top-50 markers; biased instead of unbiased; IMPACT trained 50,000 steps at batch 128 instead of 250k at batch 256 [P][C].
- **Mild holdout.** Close monocyte relatives stay in training in every M cut [R].
