## The question

- PLAN states the aim: "decompose the gap between scGen paper Fig 5b (~0.91 R²) and Bunne CellOT cross-species OOD (~0.69 Pearson r on 50 markers)" [D, PLAN:13]. The two numbers are on different scales: an r of 0.69 is an R² of 0.48 [I].
- Stage 0: does the official scGen code reproduce 0.91 with 6,619 genes and the paper's split? It was a `gate` (the repo's word for a pass/fail check): Stage 1 was not to start before r2_all ∈ [0.88, 0.93] [D, PLAN:103-115; ablation:README.md:13].
- Stage 1: with one fixed pipeline (CellOT's PyTorch "scGen" autoencoder, its `transport_scgen` prediction, one scoring script), how much of the gap comes from the training split and how much from the gene set [D, PLAN:18-39]? The plan's expected outcomes are compared with the results below [D, PLAN:284-293].
- The family is the main evidence on "is CellOT's scGen a fair baseline?". LOG0720 answers: "CellOT's 'scGen' ≠ Lotfollahi VAE — Fair for Bunne's claim (OT vs linear shift); unfair as 'beat paper scGen'" [D, LOG0720:28-29].

### The two splits

| | scGen split (`scgen`: arms A, B and Stage 0) | Bunne OOD split (`bunne_ood`: arms C, D) |
|---|---|---|
| training cells | all except rat LPS6: 52,691 cells, including all 8,421 rat control cells | mouse, pig, rabbit only: 44,270 cells |
| rat cells | control in training; LPS6 held out | none in training; split 50/50 with `train_test_split(stratify=condition, random_state=0)` into `ignore` (unused) and `ood` (scored) |
| scored: source → target | all 8,421 rat `unst` → all 9,423 rat `LPS6` | 4,210 rat `unst` → 4,712 rat `LPS6` (the `ood` half) |
| sources | [C, 02_prepare_data.py:45-49; R, ABL/logs/stage0_20260622_072217.log:62 "cells=52691"; R, manifests] | [C, 02_prepare_data.py:52-64; I, count from the table above; R, split_summary.json] |

Why the scGen split is easier [I, supported by the results below]:
- The cells to be translated are training cells. The network has learned to encode and decode rat cells, and their rat-specific baseline expression flows into the prediction. It only has to add the LPS response.
- On the OOD split (out-of-distribution: the test species is never seen in training) the encoder must also place a species it has never seen.
- The scored cells are not the cause: the do-nothing scores are almost equal on both splits (0.867 vs 0.866 at 6,619 genes; 0.660 vs 0.658 at 1,000) [R, Stage 1 table below].

Not CellOT's own split: CellOT's `toggle_ood` halves rat without stratifying by condition, and it also moves 500 cells per species × condition into a `test` set [C, MAC/cellot/cellot/data/cell.py:252-272, :313-342; MAC/cellot/configs/tasks/crossspecies-ood.yaml:13-19]. PLAN nevertheless says the split "Uses CellOT `toggle_ood` logic" [D, PLAN:196].

### Models
- Stage 0, paper scGen: TF1 `VAEArith`, a variational autoencoder (VAE: the code of a cell is a random draw, and a KL term pulls codes toward a standard normal). Layout 6619-800-800-100-800-800-6619 with batch normalization (rescaling of each layer's outputs), leaky ReLU and dropout 0.2; KL weight α = 5e-5; Adam lr 1e-3; batch 32; 300 epochs [C, ABL/scripts/run_stage0.sh:27-31; R, ABL/logs/stage0_20260622_072217.log:62 "epochs=300 batch_size=32"; C, VAE:70-107, :156-160]. The "architecture" and "training" fields of S0/metrics.json repeat these values, but they are labels typed into EVAL0, not read from the model [C, EVAL0:283-290].
  - Retrained from scratch: the authors' rat checkpoint was not in their Google Drive folder [D, ABL/results/stage0/STAGE0_LOG.md:13; R, MAC/scgen-reproducibility/gdrive/ holds only .h5ad files].
  - Upstream quirks, kept: the second decoder batch normalization's output is thrown away (VAE:103); `to_latent` returns a random sample z = μ + σ·ε, not μ (VAE:122-123, :140, :177) [C]. The local copy only adds progress logging and a loss CSV; it also rescales the validation loss used for early stopping (patience 20, threshold 0.00025), but early stopping would not have triggered in this run under either scale: at most 4 epochs in a row lacked an improvement [C, diff of ABL/ vs MAC/ `_vae.py`, VAE:384-385; I, simulated on training_log.csv]. ### Predictions
- `fig5_two_path`, the paper's Fig. 5 recipe: δ_LPS = mean z(mouse LPS6) − mean z(mouse unst); δ_species = mean z(rat unst) − mean z(mouse unst); prediction = ½(z_rat,unst + δ_LPS + δ_species + z_mouse,LPS6), then decoded [N, FIG5#c7-#c10; P, scGen Results "scGen predicts single-cell perturbation across species"]. Both δ are single-species means, so the paper's cell-type balancing plays no role here [I].
  - The same permutation indexes both clouds, so rat control cell k is paired with mouse LPS cell k in file order; the permutation only reorders the pairs. The index arrays are sized from the 7,428 mouse LPS cells, so only the first 7,428 of the 8,421 rat control cells are used, and the two δ means use only the first 7,428 rat and the first 7,428 of the 7,625 mouse control cells [N, FIG5#c7-#c10; C, EVAL0:174-185; D, LOG0723:106-108; I]. The only randomness left is the TF sampling of z [I].
  - Each predicted cell is the average of one rat control code and one unrelated mouse LPS code. Averaging two independent codes shrinks the spread of the predicted cloud (by about 1/√2 per latent axis if both clouds have similar spread) [I]. This does not affect mean-based scores but does affect MMD and JS (see the Stage 0 results in section 5).
- `fig5_average_delta`, the notebook's second recipe: δ_avg = mean z(LPS6 of mouse, pig, rabbit) − mean z(first 19,341 control cells) [N, FIG5#c6-#c8]. In the concatenation order of FIG5#c6 (mouse, rat, pig, rabbit) these controls are all mouse, all rat, 3,295 pig and no rabbit cells [I, from the counts]. Prediction = z_rat,unst + δ_avg, 8,421 cells [N, FIG5#c10; C, EVAL0:187-197]. FIG5 computes it but does not plot it [N, FIG5#c16-#c18]; it is panel d of the paper's Supplementary Fig. 9 ("All other predictions along the major linear axes of variation", scGen Results), where the authors' checkpoint scores 0.888 on all genes and 0.624 on the top-100 DEGs [P; N, SF9#c9, #c13].
  - SF9 also shows simpler one-path recipes with the authors' checkpoint: z_rat,unst + δ_LPS scores 0.914 (all genes) and 0.762 (top-100), slightly above the two-path 0.910 / 0.742 [N, SF9#c11; FIG5#c18].
- Stage 1 `transport_scgen`: decode(encode(x) + δ), with δ = mean code of training LPS6 cells − mean code of training unst cells, a plain mean with no balancing [C, ablation:scripts/06_predict_arm.py:68-81; MAC/cellot/cellot/transport.py:53-61; MAC/cellot/cellot/models/ae.py:198-208]. On the scGen split the control side is 25.3% rat and the LPS6 side has no rat; on the OOD split pig is 33.9% of the controls but 24.8% of the LPS6 cells [I, from the counts].

### Evaluation
- Stage 0 (EVAL0 + HM):
  - `r2_all`: squared Pearson r between the mean predicted and the mean real rat LPS6 profile, one value per gene, 6,619 genes. This equals the notebook's `stats.linregress` r² [C, EVAL0:227-230; MAC/scgen-reproducibility/Jupyter Notebooks/scgen/plotting.py:62-80].
  - `r2_top100_degs`: the same on the top 100 DEGs (differentially expressed genes) from a Wilcoxon rank test on real rat cells, LPS6 vs the rest (= unst), scanpy 1.4.4 [C, EVAL0:145-163; R, S0/metrics.json "paper_replication"].
  - `r2_identity`: the same score when the unchanged rat control cells are the "prediction"; `r2_self`: the score between two random halves of the real rat LPS6 cells [C, HM:159-176, :270-282].
  - MMD (maximum mean discrepancy: a distance between two clouds of cells that sees more than their means) on all 6,619 genes, samples of 30, 50 and 80 cells, 10 repeats, 50 kernel bandwidths, biased estimator; headline at 80 cells [C, HM:42-43, :66-130; R, S0/metrics.json "headline_ncells"]. `frac_gap_closed` = (ceiling − model)/(ceiling − floor) [C, HM:178-185]; the variants are explained in docs/archaeology/metrics.md §3.
  - Pass/fail checks: r2_all ∈ [0.88, 0.93] and |r2_top100 − 0.7421| ≤ 0.05 [C, EVAL0:278-279].
  - Randomness: numpy seed 0, but the TF sampling inside `to_latent` is not seeded, so repeated evaluations of one checkpoint differ slightly [C, EVAL0:88, :174; R, logs in section 5].
- Stage 1 (ablation:scripts/07_eval_unified.py):
  - Pearson r and r² of mean vectors on all genes, on the top-50 and on the top-100 markers, over all cells of the scored manifests; no subsampling or bootstrap [C, 07_eval_unified.py:57-75; D, PLAN:231].
  - The markers differ by gene set. Arms B and D read ranks stored in the file (`varm['marker_genes-condition-rank']`), computed on all 62,114 cells pooled across species [C, 02_prepare_data.py:32-42, :146]. Arms A and C have no stored ranks [I: the 6,619-gene file never gets them; the AnnData summary of train_species.h5ad in FIG5#c2 lists no varm, N] and fall back to a Wilcoxon test on all rat cells (both halves for arm C), with `reference="unst"`, `use_raw=False`, scanpy 1.11.5 [C, 07_eval_unified.py:35-44; R, dist-info in MAC/cellot/.venv]. Stage 0's top-100 list comes from the same kind of test but with scanpy 1.4.4 and its defaults [C, EVAL0:148-154]; the two lists were never compared [I].
  - The grid contains no do-nothing baseline [R, columns of ablation_grid.csv].

## Verdict
- Stage 0 reproduces Fig. 5b on all genes: 0.907 against 0.910, and a second training of the same recipe (F15) gave 0.905 [R]. The top-100 DEG match (0.7423 against 0.7421) is partly luck: the second training gave 0.694 on the same gene list [R]. Evidence: strong for the all-gene number, weak for the DEG number (two trainings).
- Fig. 5's headline is weaker than it looks. Doing nothing scores 0.867, so the model adds +0.040 (the authors' checkpoint +0.043) and closes 30% of the gap to perfect [R; I]. As a distribution, the prediction is further from the real LPS rat cells (MMD 0.201) than the unstimulated cells are (0.045) [R]. MMD on 6,619 genes has a small range (floor 0.024, ceiling 0.045), so the exact −7.25 means little; the sign is the finding [I]. Part of the penalty comes from the decoder (its own round trip of the real LPS6 cells is at 0.112) and part from the two-path averaging (see Stage 0 results) [R; I]; it does not show that the latent shift itself is wrong [I].
- Stage 1 shows that the split dominates. Holding rat out entirely costs 0.18–0.24 in r2_all at either gene set, in both the original runs and the F13 reruns, while the do-nothing scores of the scored cells barely change. This is about four to five times the observed rerun spread (≤ 0.05) [R; I]. Evidence strength: medium to strong (one run per arm, plus one rerun in F13).
- The gene-set effect is mostly a baseline effect. On 6,619 genes many genes do not respond to LPS, which pushes every score up (identity 0.867 vs 0.660 on the 1,000 HVGs) [R; I]. The r_50 and r_100 columns cannot separate gene set from marker definition (pooled vs rat-only markers) [C].
- Is CellOT's "scGen" a fair baseline?
  - On the easy split it is a fair stand-in for paper scGen on all-gene R² (0.897 and 0.882 vs 0.905–0.907) [R]. On the LPS-responsive genes it is weaker (top-100 R² 0.642 and 0.622 vs 0.694–0.742 for two VAE trainings), but the DEG lists differ slightly and the VAE's own DEG score moves by 0.05 between trainings, so the size of this gap is uncertain [R; I]. F13's recipe swap on the same autoencoder (0.617 → 0.668 at 50k steps) suggests that both the recipe and the model contribute [R; I].
  - On the o.o.d. split, the setting of the CellOT paper's claim, CellOT's "scGen" does no better than doing nothing: below identity at 6,619 genes (0.654 vs 0.866), level with it at 1,000 (0.669 vs 0.658) [R]. A plain mean shift in gene space, with no autoencoder, scores 0.901 and 0.828 on the same cells [R, AR/experiments.csv], so the loss comes from the autoencoder, not from the idea of a linear shift [I]. The authors' own numbers point the same way: on r2-means identity 0.798 > scGen 0.693 > CellOT 0.677; on MMD CellOT 0.234 < identity 0.253 < scGen 0.265 [R, species.csv]. So the paper's MMD win of CellOT over scGen is a win over a baseline that is worse than doing nothing [I].
  - Paper scGen (the TF VAE with the two-path recipe) was never run on the o.o.d. split. F13 ran the two-path recipe on CellOT's autoencoder there (0.650 on C, 0.594 on D, 50k steps, with rat control cells from both halves) [R], which did not help. Whether the real scGen would also fail is unknown; this is the missing cell of the comparison [I].
  - LOG0720's "fair for Bunne's claim (OT vs linear shift)" holds in one sense: CellOT runs in the latent space of "the same encoder that is used for SCGEN" [P, CellOT Online Methods], so the two differ only in the map. But a win over a baseline at or below doing nothing says little about CellOT [I]. Its "unfair as 'beat paper scGen'" is supported on the DEGs (with the caveats above), not on all genes [I].
- Arm D did not reproduce the CellOT authors' scGen value (r_50 0.784, r_all 0.818 vs 0.693) [R]. The arm differs from the authors' setup in HVGs, split and batch size (see Problems), so this shows no error on either side [I].
- Status "partly worked" follows from this: Stage 0 met its goal on its main number, Stage 1 answered the split-versus-genes question with one run per arm, but neither the CellOT authors' number nor the decisive comparison (paper scGen on the o.o.d. split) was obtained [I].

## Problems in how it was done
- **Stage order ignored.** Stage 1 ran on 06-17; Stage 0 first passed on 07-13 [R]. The README forbids this order [D, ablation:README.md:13]; STAGE0_LOG.md argues the tracks are independent [D, ABL/results/stage0/STAGE0_LOG.md:37]. No effect on the Stage 1 numbers.
- **Stale records.** STAGE0_LOG.md (last edited 2026-06-17) and RUN_LOG.md:32-36 still say Stage 0 is pending [D], and the GitHub copy of 04_stage0_fig5_eval.py is the crashing version [C; R, log]. A reader of the GitHub repo would conclude Stage 0 never ran.
- **Code of the final run not preserved.** S0/audit_manifest.json records the eval script as 13,497 bytes (sha256 ae7a0b…) and the audit script as 13,048 bytes (035f7c…). The files on disk are 15,367 and 15,187 bytes with other hashes [R, audit_manifest.json; my sha256 of the mirror]. Their time stamp, 2026-08-03 16:17, is shared by dozens of files in ABL/scripts/, the code files of fasrc:BK/scgen-cellot-autoresearch/ and fasrc:BK/sbatch/lps/, including stage0_metric_helpers.py whose content still matches the manifest hash, so it marks a bulk rewrite, not the date of the edit [R, file times; I]. The on-disk EVAL0 would write an extra `model_kind` field and `"alpha": 0.0` for the paper model (lines 101-105, 282), unlike S0/metrics.json [C]; alpha only enters the training loss, so the numbers should not change [I], but this is not proven. HM is not listed in the manifest, and its first commit is autoresearch@5d1a7d9 (2026-07-29), after both evaluations [G], so the HM version that produced the MMD numbers is not recorded either.
- **One TF training run in this family.** The three evaluations only measure latent-sampling noise (≤ 3e-6 in r2_all) [R]. The only retraining of the same recipe happened later in F15 and moved the DEG score by 0.048 (0.742 → 0.694) and the all-gene score by 0.002 [R, FU/round1_summary.csv]. So the near-perfect DEG match was partly luck, and the DEG check (|Δ| ≤ 0.05) would have passed the second training by only 0.002 [I].
- **"Average delta" mixes species.** Its control side includes rat but no rabbit [I, from FIG5#c6-#c8 and the counts]. The recipe is the paper's Supplementary Fig. 9d, not an invention of the notebook [N, SF9#c9, #c13]; its 0.856 (authors' checkpoint 0.888) measures a recipe the paper itself does not use for Fig. 5, so it says little about scGen [I].
- **No do-nothing baseline in the Stage 1 grid.** Without it, A vs B looks like "HVGs hurt", and arm C's drop below identity (−0.21) went unnoticed in PLAN and RUN_LOG [R; I].
- **Different markers for different gene sets.** B and D use markers pooled over all species, A and C rat-only markers [C]. This contradicts PLAN's rule "Markers computed on real treated eval cells" [D, PLAN:313] and confounds the r_50 and r_100 comparisons A↔B and C↔D [I]. The comparison of arm A's r2_100 with Stage 0's top-100 DEG R² also uses two DEG lists built with different scanpy versions and settings, never checked to be equal [C, 07_eval_unified.py:39-44; EVAL0:148-154].
- **"Bunne OOD" is not CellOT's split.** Stratified halves, no 500-cell `test` set per group, batch 256 instead of the task config's 128, derived HVGs [C]. PLAN claims "02_prepare_data.py verifies hashes match" [D, PLAN:261]; the script does no such check [C]. Arm D is Bunne-like, not a replication.
- **HVG pool includes rat control cells.** For the strict-OOD arm D, gene selection saw cells of the held-out species [C, 02_prepare_data.py:114]. Mild leakage, limited to the choice of genes.
- **Unbalanced shift in CellOT's "scGen".** On the scGen split the control mean is 25.3% rat while the LPS6 mean has no rat; on the OOD split pig is 33.9% vs 24.8% [C, MAC/cellot/cellot/models/ae.py:198-208; I, counts]. The shift thus mixes species offsets into the LPS effect. Paper scGen's single-species deltas do not [N, FIG5#c8].
- **Single runs without seeds.** CellOT sets no seed [C]; the F13 reruns differ by up to 0.048 in r2_all [R]. The reruns also changed hardware and software (A and C on GPU with a modified stack, see F13), so this spread mixes training noise with platform effects [C, AR/runs/*/train_config.yaml "device"; I]. Differences below ~0.05 inside the grid (C vs D; A vs Stage 0 on all genes) are not meaningful.
- **Irregular training.** D continued from a smoke checkpoint and was scored with a mid-run checkpoint; C crashed and resumed from step 150k [R; C]. Probably negligible, but these are not clean single 250k runs.
- **Checkpoint choice on training data.** With no `test` split, "eval" losses come from training batches [C, cellot_local_fixes.patch:33-35]. Harmless for arms that save the last weights; not for D (above).
- **MMD on 6,619 genes has almost no range.** At 80 cells the ceiling is 0.045 and the floor 0.024 [R], so `frac_gap_closed` divides by 0.021; LOG0723 notes the same for the decoded frame ("tiny usable denominator") [D, LOG0723:93-95]. The CellOT paper uses 50 markers for MMD because MMD is biased in high dimensions [P, CellOT Online Methods "Feature selection"]. The MMD of the two-path prediction also mixes three things: the decoder's own round-trip loss, the narrowing from averaging two codes per cell, and the quality of the shift [I]; "further from the real cells than doing nothing" cannot be pinned on any one of them.
- **The plan's comparison targets are uncertain.** The aim compares an R² (0.91) with an r (0.69) [D, PLAN:13]; species.csv does not record its gene set [R]; its values are bootstraps of subsampled cells, while Stage 1 scores full pools [P, CellOT Fig. 4 caption; D, PLAN:231].
- **Lost artifacts.** The Mac copies of the data and the A and C checkpoints and predictions are gone; the emptied folders carry the time stamp 2026-09-18 00:29 [R, file times; I]. ABL/results/stage1/ on FASRC holds no checkpoints [R, ssh ls]. So the original A and C models cannot be rescored. The F13 rerun checkpoints of all four arms do survive on FASRC (AR/runs/*/cache/model.pt) and can be rescored against their experiments.csv values [R, ssh ls].
- **The grid cannot test the plan's main hypothesis.** The model is held fixed in all arms, so "framework + recipe" vs "split" is only tested on the easy split, through the Stage 0 comparison [I].

## Terms used here
- **Stage 0**: rerun of the scGen paper's own code for Fig. 5, as a pass/fail check before Stage 1.
- **`gate`**: repo term for a pass/fail threshold; here r2_all ∈ [0.88, 0.93], later also top-100 DEG R² within 0.05 of 0.742.
- **Stage 1**: the 2×2 grid of CellOT's autoencoder "scGen"; **Stage 2**: the planned ICNN version, never run.
- **arm A / B / C / D**: scGen split + 6,619 genes / scGen split + 1,000 HVGs / Bunne OOD + 6,619 / Bunne OOD + 1,000.
- **scGen split** (`scgen`, "Split S"): every cell except rat LPS6 in training.
- **Bunne OOD split** (`bunne_ood`, "Split O"): mouse, pig, rabbit in training; rat cut into `ignore` and `ood` halves.
- **`train` / `ignore` / `ood`**: values of `obs['split']`; `ood` cells are scored, `ignore` cells are never used.
- **smoke / full**: 5,000 vs 250,000 training steps.
- **derived HVGs**: the locally made 1,000-gene list, not the CellOT authors' file.
- **two-path** (`fig5_two_path`): ½(z_rat,unst + δ_LPS + δ_species + z_mouse,LPS6), the Fig. 5 recipe.
- **average delta** (`fig5_average_delta`): z_rat,unst + one pooled δ.
- **identity** (`r2_identity`): unchanged control cells used as the prediction.
- **`r2_self`**: score between two random halves of the real target cells.
- **`frac_r2_closed`**: (model − identity)/(self − identity) on r2_all.
- **`frac_gap_closed`** (fgc, raw): (ceiling − model)/(ceiling − floor) with MMD; floor = two halves of the real targets, ceiling = controls vs targets.
- **`frac_gap_closed_decoded`**: the same with floor and ceiling passed through the autoencoder; the repo's `north-star`, i.e. its intended headline metric.
- **`headline_ncells`**: cells per sample for the reported MMD (80 here).
- **`r2-means` naming trap**: CellOT's `r2-means` field is an unsquared Pearson r.
- **r_all, r_50, r_100 / r2_all, r2_50, r2_100**: Pearson r (unsquared / squared) of mean vectors on all genes, top-50, top-100 markers.
- **`precomputed`**: patched CellOT split mode that reads `obs['split']` from the file.
- **`transport_scgen`, `compute_scgen_shift`**: CellOT's scGen-style prediction and its plain-mean shift.
- **`raw_shift`** (F13): mean shift added in gene space, no autoencoder.
- **`VAEArith`**: the TF1 VAE class of the scGen reproducibility code.
- **`r1_vae_drop02`** (F15): the second training of the Stage 0 recipe, with `SeededVAEArith` (`VAEArith` plus a TensorFlow graph seed).
- **`fig5_arithmetic`** (F13): the two-path recipe applied to CellOT's autoencoder.
