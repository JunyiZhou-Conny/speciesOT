Iteration 1: Notebook 04 — Held-Out T Cell (CL:0000084)
---------------------------------------------------------
File: speciesOT/baseline/analysis/04_heldout_TCell_evaluation.ipynb

Setup:
- Direction: human → mouse (later recognized as WRONG; should be mouse → human)
- scGen trained on only ~12K matched cells — far too few, led to overfitting
  (converged almost instantly, suggesting the model memorized the small dataset)
- Gene alignment: mygene text matching (less accurate than biomart orthologs)
- Held-out cell type: broad "T cell" (CL:0000084), which includes thymocytes,
  CD4+, CD8+, and other T cell subtypes — too coarse a holdout
- OOD evaluation: CellOT's default 50/50 split (52 imputed, 52 discarded),
  designed for fair IID/OOD comparison but unnecessarily restrictive for our use
- Models evaluated: scGen, CellOT (2 models only)

Results:
- UMAP: predicted cells (orange) do not overlap with actual target T cells at all
- R²: very low (poor mean gene expression correlation)
- MMD: ~0.1 (moderate; closer to 0 is better)
- Overall: poor performance, attributable to multiple compounding issues in
  the experimental setup

Issues identified:
1. Wrong transport direction (human → mouse instead of mouse → human)
2. scGen overfitting due to tiny training set (~12K matched cells)
3. Gene alignment via text matching misses many ortholog pairs
4. Holdout too broad (T cell umbrella includes many subtypes)
5. Only 52 cells evaluated due to unnecessary 50/50 OOD/ignore split


Iteration 2: Notebooks 05 + 06 — CD8+ T Cell Holdout (CL:0000625)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Files: speciesOT/baseline/analysis/05_expanded_training_CD8_holdout.ipynb
       speciesOT/baseline/analysis/06_cd8_holdout_evaluation.ipynb

Changes from Iteration 1 (documented at top of notebook 05):
- scGen now trained on ALL ~106K cells (human + mouse) excluding CD8+,
  instead of only ~12K matched cells — should substantially reduce overfitting
- Held-out cell type: CD8+ T cells (CL:0000625) instead of broad T cell,
  so model still sees thymocytes, CD4+ T cells, etc. during training
- Gene alignment: biomart one-to-one ortholog matching (paper 13 procedure)
  instead of mygene text matching — more accurate cross-species gene mapping
- All held-out cells evaluated (no 50/50 discard)
- Transport direction: mouse → human (corrected)

Three models compared:
1. scGen — vector addition baseline: encode(source) + δ → decode
   where δ = mean(target_latent) - mean(source_latent)
2. IMPACT-OR — CellOT with condition=species (mouse vs human), holdout=CD8
   Task: given mouse CD8 → predict human CD8
3. CellOT — CellOT with condition=cell_type_status (cd8 vs non_cd8),
   holdout=human species
   Task: given human nonCD8 → predict human CD8

All three use frozen scGen encoder trained on ~104K cells (excluding CD8+).

Evaluation data:
- 195 mouse CD8+ T cells (source for IMPACT-OR/scGen)
- 195 human CD8+ T cells (ground truth target)
- 195 predicted cells per model (for IMPACT-OR and scGen)
- CellOT: many more predicted cells (see UMAP issue below)

Interpretation:
- IMPACT-OR achieves the best R² on both means (0.72) and stds (0.32),
  and the lowest L2 distance on means (0.70). It is the most accurate model
  for predicting mean gene expression of human CD8+ T cells.
- scGen has the best (lowest) MMD (0.019) and near-perfect enrichment (0.51),
  meaning its predicted distribution is well-mixed with ground truth.
  However, its R² is the worst — it produces the right "shape" of distribution
  but not the right per-gene values.
- CellOT has middling R² but the worst MMD (0.069) and a suspicious
  enrichment of 0.97 (should be near 0.5 for good mixing), suggesting
  its predictions cluster separately from ground truth.


2. UMAP ANALYSIS
=================

UMAP legend (all three panels):
- Green = Actual human CD8+ (195 cells, ground truth)
- Blue  = Mouse CD8+ source (195 cells)
- Orange = Predicted human CD8+ (model output)

scGen panel:
- Blue (mouse source) forms a dispersed ring-like pattern
- Orange (predicted) forms a ring on a similar radius as blue
- Green (actual human CD8+) clusters in the center
- The predicted cells track the source distribution's shape but
  are shifted partway toward the green target — partial success

IMPACT-OR panel:
- Similar structure: blue ring, green cluster in center
- Orange (predicted) appears to move closer to the center/green cluster
  compared to scGen, consistent with IMPACT-OR's better R² scores
- Still not perfectly overlapping with green

CellOT panel — problematic:
- Massive cloud of orange dots overwhelms the plot
- Green and blue dots are barely visible (buried under orange)
- Root cause: CellOT's experimental setup uses condition=cell_type_status,
  so the "source" population is all human nonCD8 cells (thousands), not
  just 195 mouse CD8 cells. The evaluation pipeline generated predictions
  from this much larger source, producing far more than 195 predicted cells.
- The UMAP code plots all three models with the same 195 green + 195 blue
  reference cells, but CellOT's imputed.h5ad contains thousands of predictions.
  This mismatch makes the CellOT UMAP uninterpretable.
- The enrichment_k50 = 0.97 confirms the problem: predicted cells form
  their own cluster, separated from ground truth (0.5 = perfect mixing).
- To fix: either (a) subsample CellOT predictions to 195, or (b) use a
  different UMAP layout that accounts for the different source populations,
  or (c) re-evaluate CellOT in the same source→target framework as IMPACT-OR.


3. KEY OBSERVATIONS
====================

1. IMPACT-OR > scGen > CellOT for per-gene prediction accuracy (R²)
2. scGen > IMPACT-OR > CellOT for distributional similarity (MMD)
3. CellOT's experimental setup (cell_type_status condition) is fundamentally
   different from IMPACT-OR's (species condition), making direct comparison
   unfair. The two models answer different questions:
   - IMPACT-OR: "What would this mouse CD8 cell look like if it were human?"
   - CellOT: "What would this human nonCD8 cell look like if it were CD8?"
4. scGen's overfitting concern is mitigated by expanding training to 106K cells,
   but R² of 0.55 is still relatively low
5. Only 2 iterations of experiments completed so far — substantial room
   for improvement through systematic exploration

5. AUTORESEARCH-STYLE AUTONOMOUS EXPERIMENTATION (discussion)
==============================================================

Inspired by Karpathy's autoresearch (github.com/karpathy/autoresearch), which
lets an AI agent autonomously iterate on LLM training — modifying code, training
for 5 min, evaluating, keeping improvements, discarding failures, repeating
indefinitely.

Key design decisions (March 30):
- Primary search variable: cell type holdout configuration (NOT model hyperparams,
  because we need baselines comparable to original CellOT paper)
- scGen MUST be retrained when holdout changes (strict OOD: neither scGen nor
  CellOT/IMPACT may see held-out cells)
- Two metrics: R² of mean gene expression + MMD. Both must improve for "keep."
- Fixed parameters: gene alignment (biomart one2one), HVGs (1000), model
  architecture (matching original paper), optimizer settings
- Timing: ~83 min per experiment with scGen retrain, ~46 min with scGen cached
- Thymocyte strategy: rather than classifying thymocytes into CD4/CD8 (hard),
  exclude CD4 + CD8 + thymocytes together when doing T cell holdout experiments

Implementation: see autospeciesOT/ directory (program.md, run_experiment.py).
