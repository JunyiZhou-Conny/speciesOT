2. MENTOR'S NOTEBOOK ANALYSIS — E15: GENE CORRELATIONS
========================================================

Started: March 16, 2026 | Last updated: March 17, 2026

Research question
-----------------
Are top correlated orthologs shared across mice and humans? More
generally, are genetic relationships across species partially shared?

Hypothesis: A subset of orthologs within each species correlate
similarly across species, with at least 100 orthologs showing similar
correlation structure.

Method
------
1. Load all Tabula Sapiens (human) and Tabula Muris (mouse) organisms.
2. Match cells by (cell_type, tissue) using match_cells_by_celltype_tissue.
3. Query Ensembl BioMart for human↔mouse one-to-one orthologs
   (mouse_human_orthologs_biomart, defined inline in the notebook).
4. For each species, restrict to top 1000–5000 HVGs among ortholog genes.
5. Compute within-species gene-gene R² matrices (Pearson correlation
   squared) for all HVG pairs.
6. Cross-reference: for each highly correlated gene pair in species A,
   look up the ortholog pair's R² in species B.

Analyses performed
------------------
  - Clustered heatmaps of gene-gene correlations (mouse and human
    separately, then side-by-side paired heatmaps).
  - Identification of correlated gene modules (e.g., Spink4/Reg4 cluster
    specific to small intestine goblet cells in mouse).
  - Quantification: what fraction of highly correlated human gene pairs
    are also highly correlated in mouse?
  - Extraction of top mutually correlated ortholog pairs ranked by
    min(human_R², mouse_R²).

Key observations
----------------
  - The notebook defines two large helper functions inline:
    mouse_human_orthologs_biomart (~150 lines) and
    within_species_ortholog_pair_r2_tables (~200 lines). These overlap
    significantly with functions added to speciesot_helpers.py on main.
  - Conclusion is marked TBD — the analysis appears exploratory.


3. MENTOR'S NOTEBOOK ANALYSIS — E16: LEARNING WEIGHTS IN FGW
==============================================================

Started: April 2, 2026 | Last updated: April 2, 2026

Research question
-----------------
Can Fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) with optimized global ortholog
weights outperform FGW with equal weighting on a predetermined set of
ortholog pairs?

Hypothesis: Yes, optimized weights will outperform because some
orthologs are particularly informative while others are
counterproductive.

Method
------
Alternating optimization between:
  Step 1: Fix weights w → solve entropic FGW for transport plan T.
  Step 2: Fix T → update w by minimizing a quadratic-regularized
          objective on the simplex (w >= 0, sum(w) = 1).

The cross-space feature cost is:
  M_ij(w) = Σ_k w_k · d(orth_mouse[i,k], orth_human[j,k])
which is linear in w, making the weight update a convex QP projected
onto the probability simplex.

Key function: alternating_weighted_fgw_opt (defined inline, ~250 lines)
  - Uses POT's entropic_fused_gromov_wasserstein internally
  - Supports "sqdiff" and "abs" feature losses
  - L2 regularization on weights (weight_l2 parameter)
  - Convergence monitored by relative changes in T and w

Setup
-----
  - Uses 2 organisms per species (not all), matched by cell type/tissue.
  - Computes ortholog_pearson_r2_by_celltype_biomart → only 156 orthologs
    survive (those with high cross-species correlation from E14's method).
  - Extracts ortholog expression matrices for mouse and human.
  - Runs alternating optimization with epsilon=0.01, alpha=0 (pure OT
    on the weighted feature cost, no GW structure term).

Results
-------
  - The optimizer selected 7 genes (sparse weight vector):
    SFTA2, SLC34A2, CLDN18, GABRD, KCNAB3, FOXP3, SCN3B
  - Cell type pairing accuracy: 45.7%
    (vs. what baseline? Not explicitly compared in notebook, but E17
    shows standard OT gets 18.6–24.6% on the same task)
  - Conclusion marked TBD; next step noted: compare to entropic FGW
    with just top K orthologs (K=7, K=100).


4. MENTOR'S NOTEBOOK ANALYSIS — E17: STANDARD OT CELL PAIRINGS
=================================================================

Started: April 9, 2026 | Last updated: April 10, 2026

Research question
-----------------
Can standard OT couple mouse and human cells in untransformed ortholog
space (the shared 1000-HVG PCA space from our data prep pipeline)?

Hypothesis: Standard OT will couple outlier cell populations (e.g.,
non-immune cells that cluster by cell type before species) but NOT
immune populations (which cluster by species before cell type).

Setup
-----
  - Uses the cd8_holdout_v07.h5ad dataset (12,836 cells × 1000 genes).
  - Subsamples 500 mouse + 500 human cells (random).
  - Tests three OT variants in the shared PCA space:
    (a) Gromov-Wasserstein (Euclidean, epsilon=0.1)
    (b) Gromov-Wasserstein (geodesic distances, k=10 NN graph)
    (c) Standard OT via EMD (Earth Mover's Distance, no Gromov)

Results
-------
  Method                           Cell-type pairing accuracy
  GW (Euclidean, ε=0.1)           18.6%
  GW (geodesic, k=10, ε=0.1)     18.2%
  EMD (standard OT, no GW)        24.6%
  Sinkhorn (ε=0.1)                ~16% (noted in comment)

Conclusion: Only ~25% cell type pairing accuracy with standard OT on
500 cells in ortholog PCA space. Standard OT cannot effectively couple
cell types across species in this space. The mentor notes that the
graph-based approach gets non-immune cells (outlier distributions)
correct but largely fails with immune cells.

Takeaway: This motivates the need for more sophisticated approaches
(e.g., the weighted FGW from E16, or learned cross-species mappings
like CellOT/IMPACT-OR).


5. OBSERVATION — ORTHOLOG ALIGNMENT PRESERVES ALL CELLS
=========================================================

In notebook 07 (data prep for holdout experiments):

  Human combined: (58852, 61759)    ← 58,852 cells × 61,759 genes
  Mouse combined: (47802, 18024)    ← 47,802 cells × 18,024 genes

  After BioMart one-to-one ortholog alignment:
  Ortholog pairs: 14,451
  Mouse aligned:  (47802, 14451)    ← same 47,802 cells
  Human aligned:  (58852, 14451)    ← same 58,852 cells

The ortholog alignment (align_adatas_biomart_one2one) only filters
GENES, not cells. It finds 14,451 one-to-one ortholog pairs that exist
in both species' gene sets, then subsets both AnnData objects to those
genes (columns). The rows (cells) are unchanged.

This is expected behavior: ortholog matching is a gene-level operation
that puts both species into a shared gene space (14,451 paired ortholog
dimensions). Cell-level matching (which cells correspond across species)
is a separate downstream step — it is exactly what the OT methods
(CellOT, IMPACT-OR, FGW) are trying to solve.

The pipeline then applies additional filtering:
  - match_cells_by_celltype_tissue: subsets to cells with matching
    (cell_type, tissue) pairs across species
  - HVG selection: further reduces to ~1000 highly variable genes
  - scGen encoding: compresses to 50 latent dimensions

So the 58,852 human cells and 47,802 mouse cells will be reduced
significantly before OT is applied.

9. BUG FIX — `top_n_organisms_from_species` WITH n=-1 WAS DROPPING A DONOR
===========================================================================

Context
-------
While working through 01.1_hvg_investigation.ipynb, noticed that calling
`top_n_organisms_from_species(..., n=-1, species=...)` returned 23 human
donors and 39 mouse donors, which mentor had intended to mean "all
donors."

Root cause
----------
The helper (speciesot_helpers.py, previously lines 34–44) did:

    top_organisms = all_adata.obs['donor_id'] \
                    .value_counts().index[:n].tolist()

Python slicing `seq[:-1]` means "everything except the last element,"
NOT "the whole sequence." Since `value_counts()` returns donors sorted
by cell count descending, `n=-1` was silently excluding the donor with
the FEWEST cells on each side.

Ground-truth donor counts from the .h5ad files:
  sampled_human_shared.h5ad:  24 donors (58,931 cells)
  sampled_mouse_shared.h5ad:  40 donors (47,807 cells)

The silently excluded donors:
  Human: TSP13         (79 cells)
  Mouse: 3-M-8/9       (5 cells)

Any previous result (including numbers in earlier log entries) that was
generated with `n=-1` was actually run on 23 human / 39 mouse donors,
not the full 24 / 40. The 5-cell mouse donor `3-M-8/9` is too small to
matter for most downstream work, but TSP13 (79 cells) is plausibly
nontrivial.

Fix
---
Patched the helper to accept an explicit sentinel for "all donors" and
to guard against other unexpected values:

    if n is None or n == -1:
        top_organisms = donor_counts.index.tolist()
    elif isinstance(n, int) and n >= 0:
        top_organisms = donor_counts.index[:n].tolist()
    else:
        raise ValueError(
            f"n must be a non-negative int, -1, or None; got {n!r}"
        )

Jupyter gotcha encountered during the fix
------------------------------------------
After editing speciesot_helpers.py, counts in Cell 4 still printed
23 / 39 even though `inspect.getsource(...)` showed the new function
body. Cause: re-running Cell 4 only re-reads `len(human_adatas)`; the
list itself was computed by the OLD function before the edit. Module
reload updates the function object in sys.modules but does not
retroactively update already-bound variables. Fix was to re-run the
cells that CALL the helper (Cells 2 and 3), after which Cell 4 flipped
to 24 / 40.

Going forward, added the `%load_ext autoreload` / `%autoreload 2`
pattern to the notebook's header cell so future helper edits are
picked up automatically. Re-running dependent cells is still required.


10. OBSERVATION — `ad.concat(top_n_organisms_from_species(..., -1))`
=====================================================================

With the patch from §9, calling

    adatas = top_n_organisms_from_species(data_dir, -1, species)
    combined = ad.concat(adatas, join="outer")

produces an AnnData equivalent (in cell content) to just:

    combined = sc.read_h5ad(data_dir + f"sampled_{species}_shared.h5ad")

So for the `n=-1` case, the split→concat step is redundant. Minor
caveats: concat reorders cells donor-by-donor (descending cell count),
returns a fresh copy rather than a view, may uniquify `obs_names`, and
drops most of `.uns` / `.obsm` / `.layers` by default.

The pattern is NOT redundant when `n` is a bounded top-k (e.g., n=5),
because concat then yields "pooled cells from the top 5 donors," which
is a genuinely different object than the full file. Keep the split
step around for that use case; just be aware it's a no-op (aside from
the caveats above) when `n=-1`.
