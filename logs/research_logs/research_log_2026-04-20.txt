Focus:   Upstream preprocessing audit of `sampled_*_shared.h5ad` prior

1. SUMMARY OF FINDINGS
========================

Three interlocking issues were surfaced. In priority order:

  a) `sampled_mouse_shared.h5ad` and `sampled_human_shared.h5ad` arrive
     on the SAME nominal pipeline (log-normalized) but have been
     processed differently: human `.X` is `log1p(normalize_total(counts))`,
     mouse `.X` is the same PLUS an additional per-gene `/std` scaling
     with a hard clip at 10. Every cross-species analysis run to date
     has been operating on MISMATCHED scales.

  b) `ad.concat` silently drops `.layers`, `.raw`, `.uns`, `.obsp`, and
     non-shared `.obsm` entries by default. The upstream `log1p` marker
     in `.uns` and the preserved raw counts in `.raw` were being
     discarded the moment mouse and human were concatenated, which is
     why previous investigations could not confirm what transforms had
     been applied.

  c) Both `.h5ad` files carry raw integer UMI/read counts in `.raw.X`
     that survive loading but are not displayed in the default AnnData
     repr. This means the cleanest fix — re-normalize both species
     identically from raw counts — is a local operation, no re-download.

The re-normalized pipeline produces cross-species-comparable log1p
matrices. HVG selection with `batch_key="species"` now picks
biologically-sensible cell-type-specific markers. PTPRC is still
excluded, but for understandable reasons rooted in the matched-cell
composition, not a preprocessing bug (see §6).


2. CROSS-SPECIES SCALE MISMATCH
=================================

Initial symptom
----------------
While inspecting `train_combined_full.X` (the concat of human and mouse
matched-ortholog-aligned matrices):

  min=0.0000  max=10.0000  mean=0.2376   ← exact max of 10.0 is suspicious
  # unique non-zero values: 10,984,563 / 29,747,145 nnz = 37%

Splitting by species:

  human: min=0.0000  max=8.5077  mean=0.1800   (672K unique / 16.9M nnz = 4%)
  mouse: min=0.0000  max=10.0000 mean=0.2937   (10.6M unique / 13.1M nnz = 81%)

Two fingerprints diverged between species:

  * max_value: human max 8.51 is a legitimate `log1p` value; mouse max
    of exactly 10.0000 indicates a hard clip (confirmed by observing
    multiple distinct (cell, gene) pairs landing at exactly 10.0).
  * uniqueness ratio: human 4% matches the collision pattern of
    `log1p(normalize_total(integer_counts))` (many (count, cell_total)
    combos produce identical log values); mouse 81% is consistent with
    an additional per-gene `/std` step that breaks those collisions.

Verification by staircase analysis
------------------------------------
For human cell 0, the smallest non-zero value in `.X[0, :]` was 0.5677.
Solving `log(1 + k·1) = 0.5677` gives per-cell scale factor
k_0 ≈ 0.7643, implying this cell had ~13,084 raw UMI counts before
normalization to target_sum=10,000.

Predicting the cell's staircase:

  raw count | count · k_0 | log1p(...) | observed in .X?
  ----------|-------------|------------|-----------------
       1    |   0.7643    |  0.5677    |      yes
       2    |   1.5287    |  0.9276    |      yes
       3    |   2.2930    |  1.1917    |      yes
       4    |   3.0574    |  1.4004    |      yes
       5    |   3.8217    |  1.5730    |      yes
       6    |   4.5860    |  1.7202    |      yes

Every one of these predicted values appeared in cell 0's non-zero
entries with multiple co-occurrences — exactly the expected pattern
for `normalize_total(1e4) → log1p` applied to integer counts.

The same analysis on mouse data does NOT reproduce a clean staircase
because the per-gene std divisor (distinct per gene) scrambles the
values. This is also consistent with sc.pp.scale(..., zero_center=False,
max_value=10) applied on top of log-normalized mouse data upstream.

Upstream origin
----------------
  * Tabula Sapiens (human) pipeline: 2022-era CELLxGENE standard.
    `.X` = log-normalized; `.layers['decontXcounts']` = raw counts;
    `.layers['scale_data']` = scaled version kept as auxiliary.
  * Tabula Muris (mouse) pipeline: older (Seurat-v1-era) workflow.
    `.X` = scaled-and-clipped (what CELLxGENE exports for the published
    Tabula Muris release). No `.layers` retained.

Both files carry `.raw.X` with preserved raw integer counts (see §4).


3. `ad.concat` SILENTLY DROPS STRUCTURE
==========================================

Confirmed default behavior when concatenating AnnData objects:

  Attribute        Default concat behavior
  --------         -----------------------
  .X               ✓ concatenated along obs (inner join on var)
  .obs             ✓ concatenated
  .var             ✓ intersected (inner join)
  .obsm[key]       ✓ kept iff key present in all inputs AND column dims match
  .layers[key]     ✗ DROPPED (default merge='unique' does not preserve)
  .obsp            ✗ DROPPED
  .varm            ✗ DROPPED
  .raw             ✗ DROPPED (no flag to preserve)
  .uns             ✗ DROPPED (default uns_merge=None)

Concrete consequences in our pipeline:

  * The `uns['log1p']` scanpy-marker was lost when concatenating, which
    is why `log1p in uns? False` on `train_combined_full` suggested (to
    an incautious reader) that log1p had never been applied.
  * `.layers['decontXcounts']` (the human-side raw counts) was dropped
    at concat, removing one obvious path to reconstruct the raw data.
  * `.raw` was dropped silently, removing the other obvious path.
  * Of the 7 `.obsm` entries on human and 2 on mouse, only `X_pca` and
    `X_umap` survived (the intersection with matching dims) — and both
    were structurally nonsensical after concat because mouse's PCA
    coords were in Tabula-Muris PCA space and human's were in
    Tabula-Sapiens PCA space; stacking them produced a `(12786, 50)`
    array where rows 0..n_human-1 and rows n_human..end live in
    completely different coordinate systems.

Mitigation
----------
Passing `merge="same"` and `uns_merge="same"` preserves identical
entries across inputs (`.layers` survive iff both sides have the same
keys). `.raw` cannot be preserved through concat at all — must be
reattached manually after concat if needed.

Always treat post-concat `.obsm` / `.obsp` / `.uns` as stale; delete
and recompute. Any analysis that relied on post-concat `X_pca` or
`X_umap` should be considered suspect.


4. `.raw` CONTAINS PRESERVED RAW COUNTS (HIDDEN FROM REPR)
============================================================

`AnnData.raw` is a separate attribute that does NOT appear in the
default `__repr__`. Every printed AnnData summary in notebook 01.1
showed `obs, var, uns, obsm, layers, obsp` but concealed the `.raw`
attribute that was present on disk.

Inspection via h5py confirmed both files have a top-level `raw` group:

  File                           .X shape          .raw.X shape    raw is integer?
  --------------------------     ------------      ------------    ---------------
  sampled_mouse_shared.h5ad     (47807, 14451)    (47807, 18024)  yes (1.00 .. 9523593.00)
  sampled_human_shared.h5ad     (58931, 14451)    (58931, 61759)  yes (1.00 .. 5126079.00)
  tabula_muris_all.h5ad         (356213, 14451)   (356213, ~18k)  yes (1.00 .. 85.00 observed in first 1000)
  tabula_sapiens_all.h5ad       (1136218, 14451)  (1136218, ~62k) yes

The `sampled_*_shared.h5ad` files retain the full pre-processing gene
set (~18k for mouse, ~62k for human) in `.raw.var`, while the main
`.var` has been subset to the 14,451 one-to-one orthologs.

Key insight: `.raw` survives normal attribute access and AnnData
subsetting (boolean row masks, column slicing), but is dropped by
`ad.concat`. So:

  a = sc.read_h5ad('sampled_human_shared.h5ad')
  print(a.raw is None)       # False — .raw present
  b = a[a.obs.donor_id == 'TSP1']
  print(b.raw is None)       # False — survives boolean mask
  c = ad.concat([a, a])
  print(c.raw is None)       # True  — dropped by concat

This is why the per-donor splitting approach (`top_n_organisms_from_species`)
preserves `.raw` on the per-donor AnnDatas, but the subsequent
`ad.concat(human_adatas)` discards it.

Value magnitudes in `.raw.X` reflect mixed sequencing protocols:

  Protocol          Per-cell total range    Per-(cell,gene) max typical
  ----------        --------------------    ---------------------------
  10x Chromium      1k – 50k UMIs           1 – 500 (rarely few thousand)
  Smart-seq2        100k – 5M reads         hundreds to millions

Both are integer-valued, non-negative. The `normalize_total` per-cell
rescaling handles both uniformly (divides each cell by its own total),
so downstream analysis works on a mixed-protocol matrix as long as
cells are depth-normalized.


5. CANONICAL CROSS-SPECIES PREPROCESSING (NEW BASELINE)
=========================================================

Replaces the upstream `.X`-trust workflow in 01_data_preprocessing.
This is the intended path forward for all downstream CellOT / scGen
experiments on this dataset.

  import scanpy as sc
  import anndata as ad
  from speciesot_helpers import (
      align_adatas_biomart_one2one,
      match_cells_by_celltype_tissue,
  )

  MOUSE = '.../sampled_mouse_shared.h5ad'
  HUMAN = '.../sampled_human_shared.h5ad'

  mouse_full = sc.read_h5ad(MOUSE)
  human_full = sc.read_h5ad(HUMAN)

  # step 1: promote raw counts to .X
  mouse = mouse_full.raw.to_adata()
  human = human_full.raw.to_adata()
  mouse.obs = mouse_full.obs.copy()   # ensure full obs metadata
  human.obs = human_full.obs.copy()

  # step 2: cell-axis matching (before gene alignment, to reduce size)
  mouse_matched, human_matched, match_table = match_cells_by_celltype_tissue(
      mouse, human,
      cell_type_key='cell_type_ontology_term_id',
      tissue_key='tissue_ontology_term_id',
      return_match_table=True,
  )

  # step 3: gene-axis ortholog alignment
  mouse_ortho, human_ortho, ortho_table = align_adatas_biomart_one2one(
      mouse_matched, human_matched
  )

  # step 4: identical normalization on ortholog subset  (Option Y)
  for a in [mouse_ortho, human_ortho]:
      a.X = a.X.astype('float32')
      sc.pp.normalize_total(a, target_sum=1e4)
      sc.pp.log1p(a)

Ordering rationale:

  * .raw.to_adata() FIRST so every subsequent step operates on integer
    raw counts, not the mismatched processed `.X`.
  * match_cells_by_celltype_tissue BEFORE ortholog alignment because
    matching is a cell-axis op and ortholog alignment is a gene-axis op;
    matching first reduces the cell count from 58k/48k → 6.5k each,
    making the BioMart ortholog column subset cheaper.
  * normalize_total AFTER ortholog alignment (Option Y). This way each
    cell's per-cell depth correction uses the same 14,451 genes for
    both species, guaranteeing post-normalization per-cell totals
    match across species. Alternative (Option X = normalize first,
    then align) leaves a per-cell-total asymmetry because human has
    62k genes and mouse has 18k before alignment.

Verification after re-normalization
--------------------------------------
Post-pipeline diagnostic on an 11,982-cell matched+aligned dataset:

  mouse_ortho: min=0.0003  max=8.8695  mean=1.2429  #unique_nnz_sample=265
  human_ortho: min=0.0013  max=8.8025  mean=1.1957  #unique_nnz_sample=147

Both max values comfortably below 10 (legitimate log1p, no clip); both
uniqueness ratios in the same low regime (staircase collisions visible
in both). Scale mismatch resolved.


6. HVG SELECTION WITH `batch_key='species'`
=============================================

Ran HVG selection on `train_combined_full` (= concat of normalized
mouse_ortho and human_ortho) with:

  sc.pp.highly_variable_genes(
      train_combined_full, n_top_genes=1000, flavor='seurat',
      batch_key='species',
  )

Why `batch_key` matters
------------------------
Without batch_key, HVG is computed on stacked mouse+human cells. Any
gene with a species-baseline offset (e.g. uniformly high in human,
uniformly low in mouse, driven by ortholog detection efficiency
rather than biology) produces high variance purely from the species
shift and is wrongly flagged as HVG. With `batch_key='species'`,
dispersion is computed WITHIN each species independently, so
species-baseline offsets don't register as variance.

Scanpy merges per-batch HVG results using:

  1) Primary sort: `highly_variable_nbatches` (count of batches where
     gene was flagged as HVG) descending. So genes HVG in both species
     come first.
  2) Tiebreaker: average `dispersions_norm` across batches descending.

The final 1000 HVGs are the top of this merged ranking. `nbatches=0`
genes never make the cut.

Aggregation semantics of `.var` columns
-----------------------------------------
When `batch_key` is set, `.var` columns after HVG selection are
AGGREGATED across species, not per-species:

  .var['means']            = average of per-species means
  .var['dispersions']      = average of per-species dispersions
  .var['dispersions_norm'] = average of per-species z-scored dispersions
  .var['highly_variable_nbatches']     = count of species flagging as HVG
  .var['highly_variable_intersection'] = nbatches == n_batches
  .var['highly_variable']  = final top-1000 flag

Per-species stats are NOT written to `.var` by default. To recover:
run `sc.pp.highly_variable_genes` separately on species-subsets and
attach per-species columns manually.

Cell-type composition of matched dataset
------------------------------------------
The matched (cell_type, tissue)-intersected dataset ended up
IMMUNE-DOMINATED. Observed PTPRC `frac_nonzero` of 58.6% in mouse and
69.1% in human — consistent with a dataset where the majority of cells
are leukocytes. (Both atlases annotate immune cell types more
consistently than non-immune, so matching by cell-type ontology
preferentially retains immune cells.)


7. PTPRC IS EXCLUDED FROM HVGs — WHY
=======================================

Observation
-----------
  PTPRC (ENSG00000081237) in `train_combined_full.var`:
    highly_variable          : False
    highly_variable_nbatches : 0          ← NOT flagged in either species
    highly_variable_intersection: False
    means                    : 2.1317
    dispersions              : 2.9853
    dispersions_norm         : -0.2067    ← below-average for its mean bin
    overall rank             : 7183 / 14451

  mouse-only: mean=1.037  var=1.218  frac_nonzero=58.6%  max=4.702
  human-only: mean=1.677  var=1.786  frac_nonzero=69.1%  max=6.252

  1000th-ranked gene has nbatches=1, dispersions_norm=1.4664.

Interpretation
---------------
PTPRC is a pan-leukocyte marker — it's expressed in essentially all
immune cells (B, T, NK, monocyte, macrophage, DC) at similar levels.
In a leukocyte-dominated dataset (58-69% PTPRC+), it's "uniformly on"
and doesn't discriminate between cell subtypes.

In the mean-expression bin containing PTPRC (mean ≈ 2), other genes
(subtype markers like CD8A, CD19, CD14, CD68, GNLY) have SHARPER
bimodal expression — strongly on in one subtype, near-zero in others.
Their per-bin dispersions_norm is positive (+2 to +5) and they are
correctly flagged as HVGs, displacing PTPRC from the top 1000.

The algorithm's decision is correct for the goal (cross-species
cell-type discrimination in a leukocyte-heavy matched dataset): it
prefers features that distinguish subtypes OVER features that uniformly
label the dominant compartment.

If this investigation were re-run on a non-immune-enriched matched
dataset (e.g. with broader cell-type ontology matching or tissue
filters that balance immune vs. non-immune), PTPRC would likely become
bimodal and qualify as HVG. The current exclusion is a DATA
COMPOSITION outcome, not a methodology bug.

Decision
---------
No action on the HVG selection. Document that PTPRC is tracked
biologically (e.g. as a QC marker in color-coded UMAPs via `.raw` or a
preserved log-normalized layer), but is not one of the 1000 features
used for PCA / OT / scGen training. If the mentor has a specific
biological reason to force-include PTPRC, add it to an explicit marker
whitelist on top of the HVG selection.

=============================================

Assay distribution in the raw files:

  Mouse (47,807 cells):
    10x 3' v2          30,326  (63.4%)
    Smart-seq2         17,481  (36.6%)

  Human (58,931 cells):
    10x 3' v3          51,192  (86.9%)
    10x 5' v2           4,297  ( 7.3%)
    Smart-seq2          3,405  ( 5.8%)
    Smart-seq3             37  ( 0.06%)

Implications
-------------
  * 10x cells contribute UMI counts (deduplicated molecule counts);
    Smart-seq2 cells contribute read counts (amplification-inflated).
    Per-cell totals differ by ~100x between protocols.
  * Mouse is ~37% Smart-seq2, human is only ~6%. That's a 6x difference
    in Smart-seq2 representation between species.
  * `normalize_total` handles this: dividing each cell by its own total
    puts every cell on the same scale regardless of protocol.
  * For cross-species OT / scGen, the protocol asymmetry is a potential
    confound, but it's second-order compared to the scale mismatch we
    just fixed.

If downstream results later look protocol-driven rather than
cell-type-driven, consider filtering to 10x-only cells:
  * mouse 10x-only: 30,326 cells
  * human 10x-only: 55,489 cells
Simpler scale, smaller Smart-seq2-specific signal leakage. Deferred
decision; current mixed-protocol pipeline is acceptable as baseline.


9. METHODOLOGICAL / CONCEPTUAL CLARIFICATIONS
===============================================

Several concepts were re-grounded during this investigation. Keeping
condensed notes for reference:

sc.pp.scale behavior
---------------------
  `zero_center=True` (scanpy default):
    z = (x - μ_g) / σ_g per gene, then clip to [-max_value, max_value].
    Output centered at 0, clipped symmetric. Standard pre-PCA step.
    Min of output: -max_value (typically -10)
    Max of output:  max_value (typically +10)

  `zero_center=False`:
    z = x / σ_g per gene, then clip to [0, max_value].
    Output non-negative (assuming input ≥ 0), clipped one-sided.
    Min of output: 0 (if input ≥ 0)
    Max of output: max_value
    ← this is what Tabula Muris applied upstream.

Both are called `sc.pp.scale(max_value=10)`, distinguished by the
zero_center flag. The one-sided clip fingerprint is: min=0, max=exact
max_value, with multiple genes at the max.

Dispersion and binning
------------------------
Raw `dispersions = variance / mean` has a systematic DECREASING trend
with mean in scRNA-seq count data (Poisson-like noise inflates low-mean
dispersion; ceiling effects compress high-mean dispersion). Directly
ranking by raw dispersion would preferentially select low-mean dropout
noise genes.

Scanpy's `flavor='seurat'`:
  1. Bin genes into 20 equal-sized bins by mean expression.
  2. Compute per-bin mean and std of raw dispersions.
  3. Z-score each gene within its bin: `dispersions_norm`.

Positive `dispersions_norm` ⟺ more variable than peers at the same
expression level. Unit-free. Comparable across bins. The z-scoring is
what makes the selection biologically meaningful rather than a
statistical artifact of count-data physics.

Staircase of log-normalized counts
------------------------------------
For cell i with raw count total T_i, scale factor k_i = 1e4 / T_i.
After `normalize_total(target_sum=1e4) → log1p`:
  count=1 in cell i → log(1 + k_i)
  count=2 in cell i → log(1 + 2·k_i)
  count=3 in cell i → log(1 + 3·k_i)
  ...

Within one cell, non-zero values form a discrete staircase. Different
cells have different T_i hence different k_i hence different staircases.
Collisions between (cell, gene) pairs are the dominant source of low
uniqueness-per-nnz ratios in clean `log1p(normalize_total(counts))` data.

Reversing the transform: given a log-normalized value v and the cell's
k_i, recover the integer raw count as round((exp(v) - 1) / k_i).

Subcellular mRNA distribution
-------------------------------
~90-95% of a cell's mature mRNA is in the cytoplasm; ~5-10% is in the
nucleus (freshly transcribed or pending export).

Standard scRNA-seq (10x Chromium, Smart-seq2) lyses the whole cell and
captures from both compartments. snRNA-seq captures only nuclear RNA
and gives ~10x fewer molecules per cell. Your data is whole-cell
scRNA-seq.

AnnData inheritance rules
---------------------------
Subsetting rules (obs-subset and/or var-subset):
  * Obs-subset filters rows of .X, .obs, .obsm, .obsp (both axes for obsp).
    Feature-space of .obsm entries UNCHANGED (stale if you changed .var).
  * Var-subset filters columns of .X, .var, .varm, .varp.
    .obsm and .obsp UNTOUCHED — they're indexed by obs, not var, so
    scanpy has no way to invalidate them. Treat as semantically stale
    after any gene-axis change.
  * .uns is copied wholesale, not invalidated.
  * .layers is subset along the same axes as .X.

Operational rule: any time the gene set changes, delete `.obsm['X_pca']`,
`.obsm['X_umap']`, `.obsp['distances']`, `.obsp['connectivities']`,
`.uns['neighbors']`, `.uns['pca']`, and recompute. Keeping stale
embeddings causes silent correctness bugs downstream.

h5ad compat issues + fixes (baked into 01.3)

files written by the newer `anndata` used by the `01.3` notebook kernel.

      0.7 expects the flat 0.1.0 layout (integer codes as a Dataset,
      plus a shared `/obs/__categories` group).

Fix (now encoded as cells 18–19 of 01.3):
  1. Strip empty groups via `h5py` (version-agnostic).
  2. Shell out to the **CellOT env's own Python** (`~/.conda/envs/
     CellOT/bin/python`), reconstruct the AnnData from `h5py`, and
     `write()` via anndata 0.7 — which emits the 0.1.0 obs layout.

After this round-trip, all 12 `_v07.h5ad` files in
`cellot/cellot_gpu/datasets/speciesot-human-mouse-renorm/` are loadable
by both `train.py` and `evaluate.py`.

Caveats on the comparison
--------------------------
  * Stale was evaluated with `--where data_space` (original 14,451-dim
    gene space); renorm with `--where latent_space` (50-dim scGen
    embedding). Latent-space metrics tend to be more forgiving
    numerically because scGen drops most of the noise variance.
    => The absolute deltas overstate the "real" per-gene improvement;
       the *ranking* is the more defensible finding.

  * Spearman rank correlation between stale and renorm (over the 8
    group×model cells, combined R² + MMD rank): **ρ = -0.12**.
    I.e. the two families rank the experiments essentially
    independently. Group D (CD4 only) flips from worst-in-stale to
    best-in-renorm. Group C (T cell subtypes) is the only setting
    where stale slightly beats renorm.

  * Interpretation: when scaling is scientifically coherent (renorm),
    CD4/CD8 holdouts become the easy settings and the broad T-cell-
    subtypes holdout becomes the hard one — which matches biological
    intuition (fewer confounds removed means harder transfer). The
    stale pipeline's ranking was partly driven by species-specific
    scale confounds rather than by the difficulty of the OOD
    generalization.
