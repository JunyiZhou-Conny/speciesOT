## Hypotheses answered

### H1: dropout causes distribution smoothing — supported

Removing dropout:
- halves reconstruction MMD (~.114 → ~.061)
- halves mean JS (~.267 → ~.12)
- restores more variance (median ratio ~.04 → .14–.16)
- moves reconstructed zero fraction toward truth (.46 → .66; truth .74)
- moves decoded transport gap closure from ~-2.8 to approximately zero

### H2: dropout removal improves paired identity — rejected

The same change:
- lowers paired-cell Pearson²/gene from ~.087 to ~.05
- drives macro COD from ~-.02 to -.35/-.40
- lowers per-cell r from ~.878 to ~.85

Thus better cloud/marginal matching did not preserve which reconstructed row
belongs to which input cell. This is the concrete MMD-vs-pairing distinction.

### H3: KL/stochastic sampling is the main bottleneck — rejected

VAE and DAE are similar within each dropout setting. The dropout effect is much
larger than the VAE-vs-DAE effect on identity and distribution metrics.

### H4: one candidate improves identity, distribution and transport together — rejected

Dropout-0 models pass the paper transport non-inferiority thresholds and
dramatically improve distributional metrics, but fail the identity gate.
Dropout-.2 models retain the baseline identity profile but fail DEG
non-inferiority and do not materially improve identity.

No eligible winner exists.

## Important reproducibility observation

The retrained paper-recipe VAE achieved all-gene r²=.905 but DEG r²=.694,
whereas the frozen historical checkpoint has DEG r²=.742. The absolute
all-gene headline is stable; the DEG result is training-run sensitive. This is
another reason not to promote a seed-0 follow-up without an eligible winner and
multi-seed confirmation.

Two independent problems with the fraction here:

1. Sensitivity. A change of d in mmd_model moves frac by d/denominator. MMD is

2. Non-comparability. The denominator contains the scored model's own AE floor.
   Round-1 denominators were .022 (dropout .2) vs .046 (dropout 0), so -2.82 and
   -0.10 are measured on rulers of different length and must not be ranked
   against each other. Dropout-0's fraction improves 28x while its underlying
   distance improves only ~1.8x.

Correction (same day): an earlier draft of this rule claimed that
"denominator < gap_above_floor" implies |frac| > 1. That is false. The condition
is algebraically just model > ceiling (worse than decoded identity); the LPS
dropout-0 run satisfies it with |frac| = .105. Fixed in conceptual_framework.md
5.9 and the metric-atlas skill: report denominator and model/floor always, do
not rank on the fraction when AE floors differ materially, and treat
model > ceiling as an interpretation flag rather than a conditioning flag.

The atlas north-star policy is unchanged (atlas denominators .157-.225).

## Round 3 launched (identity-first)

Direction chosen by the user: keep optimizing the autoencoder, accepting the
risk that a better AE may not improve transport.

Mechanism hypothesis: the MSE objective contains no term rewarding correct
per-gene cell ordering. Round 3 adds a differentiable per-gene correlation loss
(mean over genes of 1 - Pearson r across the cells in the batch), optionally
with the per-gene log-variance term, each auto-scaled to a declared 10% of the
current reconstruction loss so no lambda sweep is needed.

2x2 at fixed z=100, dropout 0.2, 300 epochs, batch 256:
  r3_mse_b256      corr 0    var 0     <- matched-batch control
  r3_corr_b256     corr .10  var 0
  r3_var_b256      corr 0    var .10
  r3_corrvar_b256  corr .10  var .10

Batch 256 is required because a per-gene correlation over 32 cells is too
noisy; it costs 8x fewer gradient steps, so all Round-3 gates are relative to
the matched-batch control rather than to absolute Round-1 numbers.

Leakage guard: the correlation term is applied to ALL genes. The rat-LPS
top-100 DEG list comes from the held-out condition and stays evaluation-only.

Graph validated in scgen_tf1 before launch: with both fractions at 0 the
auxiliary loss reproduces the vendored loss exactly (2.9601 vs 2.9601); loss
deltas confirm the auto-scaling contributes exactly 10% per active term
(+0.2960) and 20% for both (+0.5920) of the 2.962 reconstruction loss; no
non-finite values across training steps.

### Finding 1: the correlation term did essentially nothing

pairR2 .0842 vs control .0854 (-.0013); COD +.0004 vs +.0058; DEG paired .3391
vs .3367. Directly optimizing a training-time twin of the audit metric failed
to move the audit metric.

Leading explanation (testable, not yet tested): the loss and the metric are not
the same quantity. The audit metric is computed on ONE homogeneous slice (rat
LPS6 only), so it measures WITHIN-condition cell-to-cell structure. The training
loss is computed on batches of 256 cells sampled across 4 species x 2
conditions, so its per-gene correlation is dominated by BETWEEN-group mean
differences -- which the AE already reproduces well (meanVec .96). The term is
therefore close to satisfied on arrival and exerts little pressure on the hard
within-slice structure.

Secondary contributor: Pearson is scale-invariant, so the term can be satisfied
by preserving pattern at any amplitude (varRet only .039 -> .043).

Implied fix for any Round 4: compute the correlation loss WITHIN homogeneous
groups (species x condition strata) inside each batch, not across the mixed
batch. Do not simply raise the 10% weight.

### Finding 2: the variance term behaves exactly as designed, and it is not enough

varRet .039 -> .213 (5x), zeroRecon .416 -> .660 against a true .741,
reconMMD .1127 -> .0730 (-35%), fgc -2.461 -> -1.496. But COD collapses to
-1.08 and pairR2 falls to .067.

Restoring per-gene spread without knowing WHICH cells should be high puts the
variance in the wrong places. This is the same trade-off dropout-0 produced in
Round 1, now reproduced by an explicit loss term -- so it is a property of the
objective, not of dropout.

### Finding 3: the batch-256 control was NOT undertrained

The pre-declared check passes. Control vs Round-1 batch-32 VAE dropout .2:
allR2 .9151 vs .905, degR2 .7790 vs .694, pairR2 .0854 vs .087,
COD +.0058 vs -.016. The 8x reduction in gradient steps did not degrade the
model; the control is the best paper-metric run we have and posts the first
non-negative COD in the study.

### Finding 4: our transport guardrail is tighter than run-to-run noise

DEG R2 across three runs of a nominally paper-like recipe: frozen checkpoint
.742, r1_vae_drop02 .694, r3_mse_b256 .779. Observed spread ~.085, far wider
than the .02 guardrail. Consequence: the transport-gate failures of r3_corr
(-.0396) and r3_corrvar (-.0457) are inside the noise band and must NOT be read
as real degradations. The identity-gate failures are the meaningful part of the
verdict, and those are unambiguous.

Do not treat the control's .9151 / .7790 as a new best result either; it sits
inside the same spread. Any headline claim needs the 3-seed stage.

### Decision

No winner; multi-seed confirmation is not triggered. The identity-first lever as
implemented (all-gene, batch-level correlation) does not work. Either fix the
loss to be group-stratified (Round 4) or accept that within-slice cell identity
is not reachable from this objective family at z=100 on this dataset.

### Where this points

The actionable failure is AMPLITUDE CALIBRATION on unseen cells, not pattern
learning. Next block (all inference-only on existing checkpoints):

  E1  Quantify the bias: per-gene slope of true vs reconstructed on held-out
      cells versus seen groups; is it systematic shrinkage?
  E2  Seen-fit recalibration: estimate per-gene affine (a_g, b_g) on SEEN

### E1: the failure is variance collapse, not a shrinkage slope

Median per-gene ratio of reconstructed variance to true variance, within group:

  LPS seen groups (k=7)          0.018
  LPS rat|LPS6 held out          0.040
  atlas seen groups (k=14)       0.156-0.158
  atlas CL:0000875|human OOD     0.222-0.229
  atlas CL:0000875|mouse OOD     0.000  (median gene has NO reconstructed variance)

The LPS decoder retains ~2-4% of within-group variance; the atlas decoder
~16-23% on human and essentially none on the median mouse OOD gene. This is
why within-group paired identity is low everywhere on LPS: there is almost no
reconstructed variation to correlate. Note the paired-Pearson2 values are
averaged only over genes where the reconstruction does vary, so they describe
a minority of genes on the collapsed slices.

CORRECTION (2026-07-30). The variance ratios above are medians over ALL genes,
including genes that have no true variance in that slice, so they understate
retention. This is the same fallback-dominates-the-median bug E1 itself caught
and fixed for the per-gene slope; the fix was never applied to the variance
ratio.

Mechanism, verified in code: 11_amplitude_calibration.py computes
  np.median(Hg.var(0) / np.maximum(Xg.var(0), 1e-12))
over every gene (lines 217-223). A gene not expressed anywhere in the slice has
var_true == 0 and var_recon == 0, so it enters the median as a hard 0 -- the
decoder is charged with failing to reconstruct variance that was never there.
stage0_metric_helpers.pergene_table instead sets variance_retention_ratio to NaN
where true_var <= EPS (EPS = 1e-12, lines 148-150), so the audit's median over
recon_identity_pergene.csv skips those genes.

overstates the result: it is an artifact of 32.7% (m1) / 31.1% (m2) of genes
being silent in the mouse OOD slice. The qualitative conclusion is unchanged and
still severe -- among genes that DO vary, 38.2% (m1) / 33.2% (m2) still get
exactly zero reconstructed variance, and the median retention is 2.8% / 6.8% --
4-10x worse than the same model's human OOD slice and 12-30x worse than train.
Mouse OOD reconstruction is severely collapsed; it is just not literally zero.

LPS: the two groups with a per-gene sidecar on disk
(scgen-cellot-ablation/results/stage0/recon_identity_pergene.csv, identical
slice definitions to E1's groups) are barely affected, because only 0.5-0.9% of
the 6619 genes are silent there: rat|LPS6 .0386 -> .0392, rat|unst .0390 ->
.0393, train subsample .0964 (unchanged). So the "~2-4%" LPS headline stands.
The one LPS group that likely IS affected is rabbit|unst, logged at exactly
.000 -- the same signature -- but no per-gene artifact exists for it, so it was
not quantified. The E1 seen-group summary .018 is a mean across the 7 seen
groups and therefore inherits whatever that .000 hides.

### E2: seen-fit calibration does NOT transfer; an oracle affine would fix a lot

| dataset / held-out group | var ratio | COD raw | COD seen-fit | COD oracle (LEAKY) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| LPS rat\|LPS6 | .040 | -.0212 | -.0720 | +.0719 |
| atlas m1 CL:0000875\|human | .229 | -.1015 | -.1015 | +.2832 |
| atlas m1 CL:0000875\|mouse | .000 | +.0405 | +.0441 | +.1236 |
| atlas m2 CL:0000875\|human | .222 | -.2288 | -.1941 | +.2787 |
| atlas m2 CL:0000875\|mouse | .000 | +.0359 | +.0359 | +.1248 |

Per-gene affine coefficients estimated on SEEN cells and applied to held-out
reconstructions produce no improvement, and on LPS make COD worse
(-.021 -> -.072). The oracle correction, fit on the held-out cells themselves,
moves every group's COD positive and cuts MSE (LPS .0476 -> .0400; atlas human
.1735 -> .1617).

Sanity check passed: paired Pearson2 is identical across all three calibration
arms (LPS .0989 in each), exactly as expected for an affine-invariant metric.

Reading: the held-out amplitude deficit IS affine-correctable in principle, but
the required correction is GROUP-SPECIFIC and cannot be learned from the seen
groups. So it is not a global decoder-calibration bug that a post-hoc fix
removes.

### E3 as now motivated (not yet run)

In the transport setting the source cells of the held-out cell type ARE
observed -- we see mouse OOD and predict human OOD. So a per-gene calibration
estimated on the OBSERVED source group and transferred to the PREDICTED target
group is legitimate (inputs, not targets). That is the only non-leaky version
of the oracle result, and it is the natural next test: does source-estimated
calibration improve decoded MMD and the Fig-5 metrics?

## E3 source-estimated calibration of transport predictions (jobs 35558027, 35560653)

Script: scgen-cellot-ablation/scripts/12_transport_calibration.py
(sbatch/lps/transport_calibration.sbatch). Uses SAVED predictions
(fig5_two_path.h5ad, pred_single_species_delta.h5ad); no AE or transport retrain.

Note on --seed: it seeds the MMD subsampling only. Nothing in E3 is retrained,
so a "seed sweep" here is a metric-noise replication, not a training-seed
replication. See specs/e3_devar_confirmation.yaml.

Corrections, all estimated from the OBSERVED source cells only:
  source_affine  per-gene OLS X_src ~ a*recon_src + b, applied to the prediction
  source_devar   per-gene variance de-attenuation, 1/sqrt(var(recon)/var(true)),
                 capped at 5x, PRESERVING the prediction's own gene means
  oracle_devar   same but attenuation measured on the held-out target pair

### Frame correction (first run was wrong)

The first run scored corrected predictions against UNCALIBRATED floor/ceiling.
That let a corrected prediction score below the floor (LPS model/floor 0.65) and
produced a meaningless fgc of +2.99. Corrected predictions are now scored against
references carrying the SAME correction. The correction improves the references
too:

  LPS      floor .1130 -> .0380   ceiling .1330 -> .0544
  atlas m1 floor .0495 -> .0388   ceiling .2117 -> .1168
  atlas m2 floor .0433 -> .0416   ceiling .2012 -> .1138

Also relabelled: oracle_devar matches the target's VARIANCE, which is not the
same as minimizing MMD. It is a reference point, not an upper bound -- and in
fact source_devar beats it on the atlas (1.31 vs 1.65 model/floor on m1).

### Results (each variant scored in its own frame)

| cut | variant | r2_all | r2_markers | mmd | model/floor | fgc | mean JS |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| LPS | raw | .9067 | .7423 | .2018 | 1.79 | -3.458 | .2727 |
| LPS | source_affine | .9102 | .6792 | .1703 | 4.49 | -7.047 | .3569 |
| LPS | source_devar | .9067 | .7423 | .0734 | 1.93 | -1.157 | .2036 |
| atlas m1 | raw | .8045 | .6117 | .1288 | 2.60 | .511 | .1085 |
| atlas m1 | source_affine | .6154 | .2258 | .1349 | 3.48 | -.232 | .2607 |
| atlas m1 | source_devar | .8045 | .6117 | .0507 | 1.31 | .847 | .1484 |
| atlas m2 | raw | .8032 | .5851 | .1226 | 2.83 | .498 | .1114 |
| atlas m2 | source_devar | .8032 | .5851 | .0500 | 1.20 | .884 | .1506 |

### Finding E3a: de-attenuation is free on the mean program, by construction

source_devar preserves each gene's predicted mean, so r2_all and marker r2 are
EXACTLY unchanged (.9067/.7423 on LPS, .8045/.6117 on m1). Any distributional
gain therefore costs nothing on the paper metrics. This is the property the
Round-3 variance loss did not have -- that one changed means and wrecked COD.

### Finding E3b: it works on the atlas, not on LPS

atlas m1 model/floor 2.60 -> 1.31, fgc .511 -> .847
atlas m2 model/floor 2.83 -> 1.20, fgc .498 -> .884
LPS      model/floor 1.79 -> 1.93, fgc -3.458 -> -1.157

On the atlas the corrected prediction moves genuinely closer to its own
calibrated floor. On LPS the absolute MMD falls 64% (.2018 -> .0734) but the
floor falls further, so RELATIVE performance does not improve; and the LPS
denominator is .0164, ill-conditioned by our own 5.9 rule, so model/floor is the
statistic to read there. That is consistent with the earlier flat-ratio result:
on LPS the transport map, not the AE, is the binding constraint.

### Finding E3c: source_affine is the wrong correction

It shifts predicted gene means (the b_g term) and collapses marker r2 on the
atlas (.6117 -> .2258). Mean-preserving de-attenuation is the correct form.

### Finding E3d: multivariate and marginal metrics disagree on the atlas

MMD improves substantially while mean JS worsens (.1085 -> .1484 on m1,
.1114 -> .1506 on m2). On LPS JS improves (.2727 -> .2036). So on the atlas the
correction fixes joint structure while making per-gene marginals slightly worse.
Report both; do not claim a clean distributional win on the atlas.

### Status

This is the first intervention in the whole study that improves a transport
distribution metric at zero cost to the paper mean metrics, and it needs no
retraining. It is a post-hoc decoder correction, single seed, on saved
predictions. Before any claim: repeat across seeds, and check whether it
survives on the hub AE-scGen / IMPACT v08 runs rather than only the paper VAE.

Preregistered as specs/e3_devar_confirmation.yaml (2026-07-29). Phase A is
metric-noise replication (5 MMD seeds x ncells 30/50/80 x cap 2/5/10) plus two
falsification controls the original run lacks: a gene-label-PERMUTED devar and a
single global scalar devar. If a permuted scale vector recovers most of the MMD
gain, E3 is generic variance inflation rather than per-gene calibration and the
line stops there. Phase B (gated on A) tests transfer to the hub scgen and
impact_cellot v08 runs via a new devar_frame_metrics.csv sidecar.

## Phase A of E3 confirmation: atlas cuts DONE, A1 control FAILS (job 36153557)

Spec: specs/e3_devar_confirmation.yaml. Script:
scgen-cellot-ablation/scripts/13_devar_confirmation.py
(sbatch/lps/devar_confirmation.sbatch). Inference only.
3 caps (2/5/10) x 5 MMD seeds x ncells 30/50/80 x 15 variants. All three tasks
COMPLETED (atlas ~28 min each, LPS 1h58m, peak RSS 8.7 GB on LPS).

### The effect replicates, and it is NOT per-gene calibration

Headline cell (cap 5, ncells 80), model/floor:

| cut | raw | source_devar | permuted (A1) | global scalar (A2) | permuted share of gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| m1 | 2.63-2.74 | 1.283-1.313 | 1.492-1.528 | 1.482-1.518 | .837-.856 |
| m2 | 2.65-2.80 | 1.195-1.244 | 1.388-1.450 | 1.485-1.563 | .858-.873 |

Gate outcomes, both cuts:
- A3 identity: PASS. r2_all/r2_markers move by <6e-10, so "free on the paper
  mean metrics" is confirmed exactly as claimed.
- devar improves model/floor in 5/5 seeds, and the direction holds at cap 2 and
  cap 10. So the improvement itself is real and reproducible, not MMD noise.
- A1 permuted control: FAIL. Median share of gain .852 (m1) / .870 (m2)
  against a pre-declared .50 threshold. Seed spread is +-.01, far too tight to
  be noise.

### Interpretation

A scale vector with the GENE LABELS SHUFFLED recovers ~85-87% of the model/floor
improvement, and a single global scalar recovers about as much. So E3 is
approximately "multiply the prediction's per-gene spread by ~2.2", and only
~13-15% of the effect is attributable to per-gene calibration. The E3b framing
("source-estimated calibration works on the atlas") overstates the mechanism:
what works is variance inflation, which happens to be free on the mean program
because it is mean-preserving.

This does not retract the improvement, which is robust. It retracts the
explanation. The honest claim is: the paper VAE's decoded predictions are
globally under-dispersed, and inflating their spread moves them closer to the
target under MMD; estimating the inflation per gene from the source adds little.

### LPS makes the same point more sharply (5/5 seeds, cap 5, ncells 80)

| seed | raw | source_devar | permuted (A1) | global scalar (A2) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1.788 | 1.925 | 1.189 | 0.951 |
| 1 | 1.792 | 1.933 | 1.211 | 0.968 |

A3 passes (7.9e-10). source_devar improves in 0/5 seeds, reconfirming E3b. But
the PERMUTED vector (1.19-1.23) and the GLOBAL SCALAR (0.95-0.98) both beat raw
AND beat the real per-gene correction, in every seed. So on LPS the per-gene
assignment is not merely unhelpful, it is actively worse than shuffling it.

Read the global-scalar values below 1.0 as a strained frame, not as a model that
beats perfect reconstruction: model/floor < 1 means the correction helped the
prediction more than it helped the floor cloud. Flag, do not rank.

### Combined verdict across all three cuts

The per-gene structure of the source-estimated correction is not what produces
the improvement. Atlas: shuffling gene labels retains 85-87% of the gain. LPS:
shuffling is strictly better than not shuffling. What helps is inflating the
predicted per-gene spread at roughly the right overall magnitude, which is free
on the paper mean metrics only because the operation is mean-preserving.

Per the spec, Phase B (transfer to hub scgen / impact_cellot v08) is NOT
submitted. The E3 line is closed: report it as an under-dispersion correction of
unclear mechanism, not as source-estimated per-gene calibration.

### Two measurement traps found along the way

1. A2 as first written was a silent no-op: the median of the devar scale vector
   over ALL genes returns the 1.0 fallback whenever most genes are
   unidentifiable, and 56.6% of atlas m1 source genes are (median attenuation
   over all genes is 0.000; over identifiable genes it is 0.206, giving a median
   scale of 2.20). Same trap as E1's spurious "slope ~ 1.000". Summaries of the
   scale vector must be taken over identifiable genes only, and frac_fallback is
   now reported in the sidecar.
2. oracle_devar scores WORSE than source_devar (2.32 vs 1.29 on m1 seed 0),
   reconfirming that matching the target's variance is not the same as
   minimizing MMD. It is a reference point, not a ceiling.
